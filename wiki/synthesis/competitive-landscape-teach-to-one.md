# Competitive Landscape: Teach to One

The full battlefield. Who competes for the same school budgets, instructional time, and "personalized math" positioning as TTO.

## Where TTO Sits

**Product:** Teach to One: Math (full model) + Roadmaps (digital-only)
**Scale:** ~13,000 students, 38–39 schools, 11 states (as of 2024-25)
**Structure:** Nonprofit, philanthropically funded
**Position:** Whole-classroom personalized math replacement for grades 5–9

**TTO's structural uniqueness:**
- Station rotation with 2–3 modalities per session (teacher-led, collaborative, independent)
- Algorithmic daily scheduling — assigns each student to the right modality + content
- Whole-school math block replacement (not supplementary)
- 6+ years of longitudinal empirical data per student

**No competitor replicates all four.** But several compete on subsets.

## Tier 1: Direct Competitors

*Products that could replace TTO in a school's math block.*

### 1. MATHia (Carnegie Learning)

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| **Grades** | 6–12 (direct overlap with TTO's core) |
| **Approach** | AI intelligent tutoring. Cognitive science + AI adapts at skill-by-skill level. Real-time feedback, hints, teacher dashboards (LiveLab). CMU cognitive tutor lineage (25+ years research). |
| **Scale** | Used in thousands of schools. Carnegie Learning is a $100M+ revenue company. |
| **ESSA** | Strong (WWC-validated) |
| **Price** | ~$30–40/student/year (vs TTO's higher per-school cost) |
| **Threat** | 🔴 **HIGH** — Most technically comparable AI tutor. Same grade band, same "personalized math" value prop. Software-only = easier adoption. But no station rotation, no multi-modal classroom model. |

**Why schools pick MATHia over TTO:** Cheaper, easier to implement (no classroom redesign), strong research brand, teacher dashboard is sophisticated.

**Why TTO beats MATHia:** Multi-modal learning (human + software + collaborative), daily scheduling algorithm, whole-classroom model (not supplementary).

### 2. DreamBox Math (Discovery Education)

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| **Grades** | K–8 (direct overlap) |
| **Approach** | Continuous formative assessment + adaptive learning paths. Game-like interface, conceptual understanding focus. 48,000+ data points/hr/student. ESSA Tier 1 (Strong). |
| **Scale** | 6M+ students, 600K+ educators at time of Discovery Ed acquisition (Q4 2023). |
| **Distribution** | Discovery Education reaches 50M+ students globally. Massive school relationship network. |
| **Price** | Lower than TTO; scalable implementation. |
| **Threat** | 🔴 **HIGH** — Discovery Ed's distribution is unmatched. K-8 scope overlaps perfectly. PE-backed (Clearlake Capital) for aggressive growth. |

**Why schools pick DreamBox over TTO:** Cheaper, easier implementation, ESSA Tier 1 evidence, Discovery Ed's existing relationships in 50M+ student schools.

**Why TTO beats DreamBox:** Station rotation model, teacher-led modality, algorithmic daily scheduling (DreamBox is purely software-based).

### 3. ALEKS (McGraw Hill)

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| **Grades** | 3–12 (broader than TTO) |
| **Approach** | Knowledge Space Theory (KST) — most academically rigorous adaptive system. Maps all feasible knowledge states. 25+ question adaptive assessment. "ALEKS Pie" visualization. |
| **Scale** | Decades of deployment. Bundled with McGraw Hill core curricula (Reveal Math, Everyday Math). |
| **ESSA** | Tier 3 (Promising) — weaker than DreamBox/IXL/i-Ready |
| **New products** | ALEKS Adventure (K-3, 2024), ALEKS for Calculus (2025) |
| **Threat** | 🟠 **MEDIUM-HIGH** — Trusted brand, bundled with curriculum, deep research. But lower ESSA tier, less engaging, no classroom model. |

**Why schools pick ALEKS:** Bundled with curriculum they already use, McGraw Hill brand, mathematically rigorous.

**Why TTO beats ALEKS:** Multi-modal, classroom-based, daily scheduling, more engaging.

### 4. i-Ready Personalized Instruction (Curriculum Associates)

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| **Grades** | K–12 (i-Ready Pro specifically targets middle school) |
| **Approach** | Integrated assessment → instruction loop. Diagnostic places student, adaptive lessons target gaps. Also offers core curriculum (i-Ready Classroom Mathematics). |
| **Scale** | ~40% of US schools already use i-Ready. Curriculum Associates is a ~$2B+ private company. |
| **ESSA** | Tier 2 (Moderate) for core math; Tier 2-3 for personalized instruction |
| **State adoptions** | Texas IMRA approved (2024) |
| **Threat** | 🔴 **HIGH** — Already in 40% of schools. Assessment-to-instruction loop is extremely sticky. Schools already paying may see TTO as redundant. |

**Why schools pick i-Ready:** Already installed, assessment → instruction integration, proven at scale, EdReports all-green.

**Why TTO beats i-Ready:** i-Ready is primarily adaptive practice, not personalized classroom redesign. No station rotation, no multi-modality.

### 5. IXL Math

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| **Grades** | PK–12 |
| **Approach** | 17,000+ adaptive skills. Real-time diagnostic + personalized recommendations. SmartScore (0-100 mastery). |
| **Scale** | 18M+ students, 70K+ schools. Private and profitable. |
| **ESSA** | Tier 1 (Strong) — Johns Hopkins RCT (2023), ES=+0.13 |
| **Price** | Low cost, easy to implement |
| **Threat** | 🟠 **MEDIUM-HIGH** — Ubiquitous. But primarily supplementary practice, not whole-classroom replacement. |

**Why schools pick IXL:** Cheap, ubiquitous, easy, ESSA Tier 1.

**Why TTO beats IXL:** IXL is practice-based, not personalized instruction. No station rotation, no scheduling, no classroom model.

### 6. Khan Academy + Khanmigo

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| **Grades** | 3–Geometry (Learning Paths aligned to MAP Growth) |
| **Approach** | Free core product + Khanmigo AI tutor ($4/student/mo). GPT-4 powered. Learning Paths = personalized math based on NWEA MAP scores. Google Gemini + Microsoft Azure partnerships. |
| **Scale** | 700K+ student/teacher users (up from 68K), 380+ districts (up from 45). Explosive growth trajectory. |
| **ESSA** | Tier 3 (Promising) — +22 points on state math assessment with 30 min/week usage. Khanmigo has no ESSA rating yet. |
| **Price** | Core = free. Khanmigo = $4/student/month. |
| **Threat** | 🔴 **HIGH and RISING** — Free + AI + Google/Microsoft backing + explosive growth. As Khanmigo matures, it becomes the "good enough" free alternative. |

**Why schools pick Khan Academy:** Free, trusted brand, Google/Microsoft partnerships, AI tutoring included.

**Why TTO beats Khan Academy:** No station rotation, no classroom model, no multi-modality, AI-only (no human teacher component), limited longitudinal data.

### 7. Zearn Math

| Dimension | Details |
|---|---|
| **Grades** | K–8 (stronger in elementary; limited middle school) |
| **Approach** | Free core curriculum with adaptive digital lessons. Tower of Power assessment component. |
| **Scale** | 1M+ students, strong in elementary |
| **ESSA** | Tier 1 (Strong) — meets WWC standards without reservations |
| **Threat** | 🟡 **MEDIUM** — Free, Tier 1 evidence, but limited middle school presence. More of an elementary competitor. |

## Moat Analysis: What TTO Has That Others Can't Replicate

### Structural Moats (Hard to Copy)

| Moat | Description | Replicability |
|---|---|---|
| **Station rotation operating system** | Algorithmic daily scheduling across 2-3 modalities, assigning each student to right modality + content | 🔒 Very hard — requires classroom redesign, teacher training, operational infrastructure |
| **Multi-modal learning model** | Teacher-led + collaborative + independent stations in every session | 🔒 Hard — pure software competitors can't add human instruction |
| **6-year longitudinal data** | Per-student empirical decay curves, cohort priors, per-skill forgetting patterns | 🔒 Very hard — requires years of deployment at scale |
| **Whole-classroom replacement model** | Replaces traditional math block entirely, not supplementary | 🔒 Hard — requires school commitment and operational change |

### Transient Advantages (Copyable)

| Advantage | Description | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Three-Ring mastery system** | New/Practice/Maintain decomposition | 🟡 MATHia, ALEKS, Khan Academy all have mastery models. The specific ring metaphor is unique but the concept is not. |
| **BrainBreak metacognitive data** | Emotion + confidence at session boundaries | 🟡 Eedi captures confidence per question. If Eedi or others add emotion data, TTO's advantage narrows. |
| **Empirical decay (Path B)** | 6 years of real forgetting data vs algorithmic approximation | 🟡 Duolingo's HLR and Anki's FSRS are algorithmically superior but lack TTO's domain-specific data. Data advantage decays if competitors accumulate similar data. |
| **Roadmaps digital product** | Scalable diagnostic + learning path | 🟡 Directly competes with Khan Academy Learning Paths, i-Ready diagnostic, ALEKS — all larger, cheaper, more distributed. |

### Moat Gaps (What TTO Lacks)

| Gap | What Competitors Have | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **ESSA Tier 1-2 evidence (full model)** | IXL, DreamBox, Zearn all have Tier 1 | 🔴 Procurement blocker |
| **Scale / distribution** | Discovery Ed (50M+), i-Ready (40% of US schools), Khan Academy (free) | 🔴 Growth limiter |
| **Price competitiveness** | Khan Academy (free), IXL (low), Third Space Learning (~$5K/school) | 🟠 Budget-constrained districts |
| **Core curriculum adoption** | Savvas enVision dominating state adoptions (CA, SC, KY, VA) | 🟠 TTO is supplemental/replacement, not state-adopted core |

## Where the Market Is Heading (2025-2030)

1. **AI personalization becomes table stakes** — every player adding AI tutoring (Khanmigo, ALEKS for Calculus, DreamBox enhancements)
2. **Platform consolidation** — districts want fewer vendors; assessment + instruction + data in one platform wins (Curriculum Associates, Discovery Education best positioned)
3. **Evidence requirements intensify** — post-ESSER, districts must justify every dollar; ESSA Tier 1-2 becomes procurement filter
4. **Free disruption** — Khan Academy (free) + Khanmigo ($4/student/mo) threatens all paid competitors
5. **Middle school is the hardest segment** — content complexity + adolescent engagement + teacher shortages = highest need, hardest to serve
6. **State adoptions drive core curriculum** — Savvas enVision dominating; TTO bypasses this channel (supplemental/replacement, not state-adopted core)

## Strategic Implications for TTO

### Defend the Moat

1. **Get ESSA Tier 1-2 evidence for the full model** — this is the #1 procurement gap. Without it, TTO loses to IXL/DreamBox in district RFPs.
2. **Leverage the 6-year data asset** — no competitor can replicate this quickly. Use it to prove superior outcomes, not just describe the model.
3. **Double down on what software can't do** — station rotation, multi-modal learning, teacher orchestration. These are structural moats.

### Build New Moats

4. **Teacher nudge system** — research-backed (+1.89% math progress at N≈3M), no competitor ships it. First permanent behaviorally-designed teacher nudge system.
5. **Metacognitive layer** — BrainBreak already captures emotion + confidence. Adding planning prompts (ES=1.11) and confidence × performance divergence signals creates a metacognitive data layer no competitor has.
6. **Insight Recall system** — Pure white space. No one combines student insight capture + contextual recall in K-12 math. Genuinely novel.

### Watch the Flanks

7. **Khanmigo maturation** — Free + AI + Google/Microsoft. If it reaches "good enough" quality, budget-constrained districts will default to free. TTO must prove dramatically better outcomes to justify the cost differential.
8. **Third Space Learning pricing** — ~$5,000/school vs TTO's significantly higher cost. If Skye AI quality is adequate, this undercuts TTO in price-sensitive districts.
9. **Discovery Education bundling** — DreamBox + DE Experience + professional development = comprehensive district solution. Hard to compete against a bundle.
