# Self-Determination Theory (SDT)

**Self-Determination Theory** (Deci & Ryan) posits that human motivation is driven by three basic psychological needs: **autonomy** (feeling volitional and self-directed), **competence** (feeling effective and capable), and **relatedness** (feeling connected to others). When these needs are satisfied, intrinsic motivation flourishes and learning outcomes improve. SDT has the strongest intervention evidence base among motivation frameworks relevant to math education.

## Three Basic Needs

| Need | Definition | Classroom Expression | Digital Expression |
|------|-----------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Autonomy** | Feeling volitional, self-directed | Student choice in tasks, pacing, approach | Adjustable difficulty, self-directed pathways, meaningful choices |
| **Competence** | Feeling effective, capable | Appropriate challenge, clear feedback, mastery experiences | Scaffolded progression, immediate feedback, skill-building |
| **Relatedness** | Feeling connected, belonging | Teacher-student rapport, peer collaboration, community | Collaborative features, teacher presence, social recognition |

## Meta-Analytic Evidence

[[paper-sdt-meta-analysis-2024|Wang et al. (2024)]] — 36-study meta-analysis of SDT-based educational interventions:

- **Intrinsic motivation**: significantly improved
- **Perceived autonomy**: significantly improved
- **Perceived competence**: significantly improved
- **Key finding**: **Autonomy support is the critical active ingredient** — interventions targeting autonomy show the strongest effects

This is the strongest evidence base among motivation theories for educational intervention. Contrast with [[growth-mindset]] interventions which show near-zero effects when rigorously controlled.

## Longitudinal Outcomes

[[paper-sdt-math-outcomes-2026|Santiago et al. (2026)]] found that secondary school students whose math classes satisfied SDT needs had:

- **Lower math anxiety** as college undergraduates
- **Higher likelihood of choosing STEM majors**
- More positive emotional memories of math class

The effects of SDT need-satisfaction in secondary school persist into college — this is not a short-term boost but a lasting protective factor.

## Digital Learning Challenges

[[paper-sdt-online-math-2024|Shank et al. (2024)]] tested SDT in an online math unit:

- **Competence**: significantly improved ✅
- **Relatedness**: significantly improved ✅
- **Autonomy**: less evident quantitatively ⚠️

**Key design warning**: Autonomy support does not emerge automatically in digital environments even when competence and relatedness are well-supported. It requires intentional scaffolding — student choice, self-directed pacing, meaningful options.

## Implications for Teach to One / New Classrooms

1. **Prioritize autonomy support** — It's the active ingredient. Features that give students meaningful choice (pathway selection, problem approach, pacing) should be high priority
2. **Design for all three needs** — Competence through appropriate challenge and feedback, relatedness through collaborative and teacher-connected features, autonomy through student agency
3. **Invest extra in digital autonomy** — Don't assume competence and relatedness features will automatically create autonomy. Explicitly design for student self-direction
4. **Frame as long-term investment** — SDT need-satisfaction has lasting effects on STEM trajectories, not just short-term engagement
5. **Use SDT over growth mindset** — SDT interventions have far stronger evidence than [[growth-mindset]] interventions

## CBL and SDT Need-Satisfaction

Competency-based learning naturally satisfies all three SDT needs, making it a structurally motivation-aligned approach:

- **Autonomy** — students have choice in pace and pathway, advancing when they demonstrate mastery rather than on a fixed schedule
- **Competence** — mastery-gated advancement ensures students always work at an appropriate challenge level, with clear evidence of progress
- **Relatedness** — teacher-student relationships are reoriented toward individualized support and coaching rather than whole-class delivery

This alignment means CBL does not require bolted-on motivation interventions — the structure itself supports intrinsic motivation. However, as Shank et al. (2024) caution, autonomy support in digital environments does not emerge automatically and requires intentional design (see [[differentiated-instruction]] for how CBL operationalizes this).
