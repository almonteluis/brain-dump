# Culturally Responsive Pedagogy

Culturally responsive pedagogy (CRP) is Gloria Ladson-Billings' (1995) framework that starts from a different premise than most education reform: students' cultural backgrounds are *assets* for learning, not deficits to overcome. Not "these kids are behind and we need to catch them up." Rather, "these kids come with knowledge and ways of knowing that we should build on."

## The Three Pillars

| Pillar | What it means | In the classroom |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Academic Achievement** | All students held to high intellectual standards, supported in meeting them | Rigorous content, high expectations, appropriate scaffolding |
| **Cultural Competence** | Students develop competence in their own culture and at least one other | Diverse perspectives in curriculum, student cultural knowledge as resource |
| **Sociopolitical Consciousness** | Students learn to identify, analyze, and address social inequities | Critical inquiry, community connections, relevance to lived experience |

## In Competency-Based Learning

CBE and CRP are natural allies, and the Competency Collaborative's work in NYC schools shows why:

- **Transparent grading** — students know exactly what they need to demonstrate, reducing the ambiguity that disadvantages students unfamiliar with academic norms
- **Redo/revise** — revision opportunities benefit students who need multiple attempts due to language barriers, test anxiety, or different preparation
- **Flexible pacing** — no penalty for needing more time; no holding back for advancing faster
- **Multiple pathways to mastery** — students demonstrate competence through different modalities, valuing diverse forms of knowledge

The Aurora Institute (2020) adds: transparent learning targets, flexible pacing, multiple pathways, and culturally sustaining pedagogy. These aren't separate initiatives — they're the same idea from different angles.

## Design Implications for CBL Platforms

1. **Multiple representations of math ideas** — let students approach problems using different cultural and experiential frameworks
2. **Culturally relevant problem contexts** — problem scenarios reflect diverse experiences, not just dominant cultural narratives
3. **Transparent criteria** — mastery rubrics clear and accessible to students from all backgrounds
4. **Flexible pacing without stigma** — slower pacing ≠ lower expectations
5. **Multiple demonstration pathways** — show mastery through different modalities

The open thread: CRP asks "whose knowledge counts?" Most adaptive learning systems answer that question implicitly through their problem contexts, examples, and assessment formats. If every word problem features a suburban kid buying baseball cards, the system is making a cultural statement whether it intends to or not. The question isn't whether to be culturally responsive — it's whether you're doing it intentionally or accidentally.
