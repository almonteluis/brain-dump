# Performance-Based Assessment

Performance-based assessment evaluates students by having them **demonstrate what they can actually do** through authentic tasks, portfolios, and structured evaluations — rather than measuring recall on a standardized test. It's the primary assessment modality in [[competency based learning]] systems, and it rests on a simple principle from Stiggins (2005): assessment must *serve* learning, not just measure it.

## The Key Frameworks

### Stiggins (2005) — Student-Involved Assessment
Assessment should be student-involved: portfolios, performance tasks, and self-reflection become evidence of competency. Students who understand the learning targets and participate in their own assessment develop stronger self-regulation. The shift: from teacher-done-to-student to collaborative process.

### Tobón, Pimienta & García Fraile (2020) — CBA Rubric Design
Rubric design must address: integration of learning domains (not just cognition), authenticity (real-world tasks, not artificial test conditions), interdisciplinarity, student progression tracking over time, and field expert involvement in determining evaluation indicators.

### Stefanakis (2002) — Portfolio Assessment
Portfolios as comprehensive alternative to standardized testing: diverse evidence types (written work, projects, presentations, reflections), accommodates multiple intelligences, captures growth over time rather than snapshots.

## Multiple Evidence Types

| Evidence Type | What it looks like | Example |
|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Performance tasks** | Authentic problems requiring application | Real-world math scenarios |
| **Portfolios** | Curated work over time | Growth portfolios showing progression |
| **Teacher observation** | Professional judgment of demonstrated skill | Classroom interaction, problem-solving process |
| **Self-reflection** | Student assessment of own learning | Metacognitive journal entries |
| **AI-assisted analysis** | Automated evidence from interaction data | Knowledge tracing, solution path analysis |
| **Peer assessment** | Structured evaluation by classmates | Rubric-guided peer review |

## Rubric Design Principles

1. **Define mastery clearly** — students know what "done" looks like before starting
2. **Address multiple dimensions** — reasoning, communication, persistence, not just correctness
3. **Support growth tracking** — distinguish between novice, developing, proficient, advanced
4. **Co-construct** — involving students in rubric development increases understanding and buy-in
5. **Include affective dimensions** — especially for non-cognitive outcomes (see [[reverse-scoring]] for psychometric validity)

## Connection to Skill Trees

In [[skill-tree-competency-structure]] systems, performance-based assessment maps onto the competency DAG naturally:
- Each node has associated performance criteria
- Evidence accumulates at each node as students demonstrate mastery
- The DAG structure enables automated gap detection
- Progress is gated by demonstrated performance, not time

The open thread: performance-based assessment scales poorly. One teacher reviewing 30 portfolios is manageable. One teacher reviewing 150 portfolios with multiple evidence types per student is not. AI-assisted analysis can help, but who trusts an algorithm to evaluate a student's metacognitive reflection? The tension between scalability and authenticity is where [[formative-assessment]] and [[knowledge-tracing]] do the heavy lifting — continuous, automated evidence collection that doesn't require a teacher to grade every artifact.
