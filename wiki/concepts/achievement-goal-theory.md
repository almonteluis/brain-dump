# Achievement Goal Theory

**Achievement goal theory** (Ames, Dweck, Elliot) describes why students engage in learning tasks based on the goals they pursue. The two fundamental goal orientations are **mastery goals** (focus on learning, understanding, and improvement) and **performance goals** (focus on demonstrating competence relative to others). Modern frameworks further distinguish between **approach** and **avoidance** variants of each.

## Goal Types

| Goal Type | Focus | Behavioral Pattern |
|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| **Mastery-approach** | Learning, understanding, growth | Deep processing, persistence, help-seeking |
| **Mastery-avoidance** | Avoiding misunderstanding | Anxiety about not learning enough |
| **Performance-approach** | Outperforming others | Strategic effort, but superficial when stakes are high |
| **Performance-avoidance** | Avoiding appearing incompetent | Self-handicapping, help-avoidance, disengagement |

## Profile-Based Approaches

[[paper-achievement-goal-profiles-math-2023|Chan & Liem (2023)]] used latent profile analysis to identify distinct goal profiles in mathematics:

- **Mastery-oriented profiles** → higher achievement, self-efficacy, lower anxiety
- **Performance-oriented profiles** → mixed outcomes, higher anxiety
- **Multi-goal profiles** → complex patterns challenging simplistic framing

[[paper-evt-achievement-goals-2025|Xu (2025)]] integrated achievement goals with [[expectancy-value-theory]] and [[self-regulated-learning|self-regulation]] to identify six homework motivation profiles among 1,256 students. This multi-theory approach captured more variance than any single framework.

## Instructional Quality Shapes Goal Adoption

[[paper-achievement-goal-profiles-math-2023|Chan & Liem]] found that class-level instructional quality moderates which profiles students adopt:

- **Cognitive activation** → promotes mastery-oriented profiles
- **Supportive climate** → supports multi-goal and mastery profiles
- **Classroom management** → enables the conditions for mastery focus
- **Instructional clarity** → reduces performance-avoidance

**Key insight**: Goal profiles are not fixed individual traits — they are shaped by the instructional environment. This means product design and teacher practices can shift students toward more adaptive profiles.

## Teacher Positioning

[[paper-teacher-positioning-math-identity-2023|Canonigo & Joaquin (2023)]] showed that teacher positioning influences [[math-identity]] through problem-solving flexibility, connecting social positioning theory with goal adoption.

## Implications for Teach to One / New Classrooms

1. **Assess goal orientations** — Understand whether students are mastery- or performance-oriented to tailor interventions
2. **Design for mastery** — Emphasize learning, growth, and understanding over comparison and ranking
3. **Avoid pure performance framing** — Leaderboards and competitive elements can push students toward performance-avoidance
4. **Leverage instructional quality** — Teacher-facing features that support cognitive activation and supportive climate can shift goal profiles
5. **Support multi-goal students** — Some students benefit from combining mastery and performance-approach goals
6. **Integrate with EVT and SRL** — No single theory captures full motivation complexity
