# Math Identity

**Math identity** is a student's sense of themselves as a math learner and doer — encompassing their beliefs about whether they "are a math person," their sense of belonging in mathematical contexts, and their view of math as relevant to their present and future self. It is a multidimensional construct that bridges cognitive (beliefs about ability), affective (feelings about math), and social (recognition by others) dimensions.

## Measurement

[[paper-math-identity-instrument-2023|Cribbs & Utley (2023)]] developed the first validated group-administrable math identity instrument spanning grades 5–12:

- Addresses a significant gap: prior measures lacked validity evidence for efficient group administration
- Multidimensional design captures the full construct
- Efficient enough for practical use in educational settings
- Enables longitudinal tracking of identity development

For Teach to One, this instrument could inform how the platform measures changes in students' math identity over time as a key outcome metric.

## Teacher Positioning as Identity-Shaping Mechanism

[[paper-teacher-positioning-math-identity-2023|Canonigo & Joaquin (2023)]] found that **teacher positioning** — how teachers frame students' roles and capabilities — significantly shapes math identity:

```
Teacher Positioning → Problem-Solving Flexibility → Math Identity
```

Key insights:
- Math identity is **co-constructed** through teacher-student interactions, not purely internal
- When teachers position students as capable mathematical thinkers, students develop greater problem-solving flexibility
- Classroom discourse is an identity construction mechanism
- Problem-solving flexibility partially mediates the pathway

## Connection to STEM Engagement

[[paper-evt-math-profiles-2024|Mayerhofer et al. (2024)]] showed that math motivation profiles predict STEM attitudes and engagement, suggesting identity-relevant outcomes. [[paper-math-anxiety-stem-choices-2024|Cuder et al. (2024)]] found that the anxiety–self-efficacy interplay determines STEM trajectory, where identity plays a mediating role.

## Distinction from Related Constructs

[[paper-growth-mindset-differentiation-2025|Xu & Dieckmann (2025)]] emphasizes that mathematical mindset (domain-specific beliefs about math ability) is distinct from general growth mindset and self-efficacy. Math identity overlaps with mathematical mindset but is broader — it includes social recognition, belonging, and future relevance, not just beliefs about malleability.

## Implications for Teach to One / New Classrooms

1. **Measure identity as an outcome** — Use validated instruments to track identity development, not just achievement
2. **Design for positive positioning** — Teacher-facing features could include positioning-aware language suggestions and feedback templates
3. **Build problem-solving flexibility** — This is a concrete lever for strengthening identity; offer multiple solution paths
4. **Address the social dimension** — Identity is co-constructed; features that foster peer recognition and teacher connection matter
5. **Connect math to future self** — Identity includes relevance; help students see math's role in their futures
6. **Separate from growth mindset** — Don't conflate identity-building with mindset messaging

## Teacher Positioning in CBL Contexts

CBL environments create unique opportunities for identity-positive teacher positioning:

- **Data-informed positioning** — competency data allows teachers to position students as capable based on demonstrated mastery, not assumptions
- **Individualized discourse** — in CBL's small-group and station-rotation models, teachers can engage in the sustained, individualized discourse that Canonigo & Joaquin (2023) found drives identity co-construction
- **Growth-oriented framing** — mastery-gated advancement naturally frames all students as growing, regardless of starting point
- **Multiple competence demonstrations** — CBL's multiple pathways to mastery allow students to show mathematical competence in ways that align with their strengths, broadening the basis for identity affirmation

The key insight from the [[cbl-differentiated-support]] research is that **identity is co-constructed, not self-discovered** — CBL must therefore design for teacher-student interaction quality, not just content delivery efficiency. See [[culturally-responsive-pedagogy]] for how cultural affirmation further strengthens identity development.
