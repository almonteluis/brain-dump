# Competency-Based Learning

**Competency-Based Learning (CBL)** is an educational paradigm where student advancement is determined by **demonstrated mastery of defined competencies** rather than time spent in class (seat-time). Students progress at flexible paces along transparent learning pathways, with assessment embedded as a continuous, supportive process.

## Three Pillars

CBL rests on three interdependent pillars, each directly informing the [[Operation Endgame]] platform design:

| Pillar | Definition | Operation Endgame Mapping |
|--------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Transparent learning targets** | Students know what mastery looks like before they demonstrate it | Skill tree with verifiable DAG properties — [[skill-tree-competency-structure]] |
| **Mastery-gated progression** | Advancement requires demonstrated competency, not time served | Three-ring mastery stages (New → Practice → Mastered → Maintained) — [[three-ring-mastery-system]] |
| **Continuous, flexible assessment** | Multiple evidence types, low-stakes, formative — replaces single-point-in-time tests | Formative assessment engine + [[adaptive-feedback-education]] |

## How CBL Differs from Traditional Education

| Dimension | Traditional | Competency-Based |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Progress metric** | Seat-time, grade level | Demonstrated mastery |
| **Pacing** | Fixed (all students move together) | Flexible (each student at their own pace) |
| **Assessment** | Summative, high-stakes, end-of-unit | Continuous, low-stakes, formative |
| **Evidence** | Single test scores | Multiple evidence types (performance tasks, portfolios, self-reflection) |
| **Equity** | One pathway | Multiple pathways to demonstrate mastery |
| **Failure mode** | Students advance unprepared | Students stay until ready |

## Connection to Self-Determination Theory

CBL's design principles align strongly with [[self-determination-theory]] (SDT):

- **Autonomy** — Flexible pacing, multiple pathways to demonstrate mastery, student choice in evidence types
- **Competence** — Mastery-gated progression ensures students only advance when genuinely ready; formative feedback builds self-efficacy
- **Relatedness** — Transparent rubrics and shared learning targets create common language between students and teachers

The Aurora Institute (2020) and Competency Collaborative (2023) research shows that CBL's equity benefits — particularly for marginalized students — are partly explained by these SDT-aligned design choices.

## Key Researchers and Frameworks

- **Tobón, Pimienta & García Fraile (2020)** — Formalized [[performance-based-assessment]] rubric design for CBA, emphasizing integration across learning domains, authenticity, and interdisciplinary assessment
- **Stiggins (2005)** — Student-involved assessment; portfolios and self-reflection as evidence of competency
- **Black & Wiliam (1998)** — [[formative-assessment]] as the engine of CBL; meta-analysis showing formative assessment among the most powerful interventions
- **Hattie (2009)** — Effect sizes validating CBL's approach: formative evaluation (d = 0.90), feedback (d = 0.73), mastery learning (d = 0.57)
- **Aurora Institute (2020)** — Equity framework for CBL assessment systems
- **García-Martínez et al. (2023)** — CBL + formative assessment feedback for soft skills acquisition
