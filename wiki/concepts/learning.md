# Learning

Learning is the process of acquiring knowledge, skills, or understanding through experience, study, or instruction. Every field has its own definition and none of them fully captures it. The psychologists have spent 100+ years arguing about what's happening when someone learns, and they've produced four major paradigms — each one right about something important and blind to something the next paradigm caught.

## The Four Paradigms

| Paradigm | Learning is... | Gets right | Misses |
|----------|---------------|-----------|--------|
| **Behaviorism** (Skinner) | Behavior change through reinforcement | Feedback matters. Repetition matters. | Inner experience, understanding, transfer |
| **Cognitivism** (Piaget, Bruner) | Mental model construction | [[cognitive-load]] is real. Memory has structure. | Social context, culture, motivation |
| **Constructivism** (Vygotsky) | Active construction of knowledge in social context | [[zone-of-proximal-development]], [[scaffolding]], the social piece | Can slide into "just let them discover everything" |
| **Connectivism** (Siemens) | Network formation — connecting to knowledge, not storing it | Relevance to the internet age | Still thin on empirical evidence |

None of these is "right." They're lenses. A kid drilling multiplication facts is in behaviorist territory. A kid grappling with [[productive-struggle]] through a hard problem is in constructivist territory. A kid building a [[knowledge-graph-education|knowledge graph]] of how fraction concepts connect is in cognitivist territory. Same kid, same classroom, different paradigms for different moments.

## Why This Matters for the Vault

Every concept in this wiki implicitly sits inside one or more of these paradigms:

- [[spaced-repetition]] → behaviorist mechanics + cognitivist memory models
- [[knowledge-tracing]] → behaviorist (it tracks observable responses) + cognitivist (it infers hidden states)
- [[productive-struggle]] → constructivist
- [[master-based learning]] → behaviorist framework with cognitivist goals
- [[insight-recall]] → constructivist metacognition

The thread worth pulling: [[project-atlas]] has to operate across all four paradigms simultaneously. The scheduling is behaviorist. The [[skill-graph]] is cognitivist. The [[productive-struggle]] design is constructivist. The networked knowledge representation is connectivist. Most ed-tech picks one paradigm and optimizes for it. The ambitious play is building a system that knows *which paradigm applies when* and adapts accordingly.
