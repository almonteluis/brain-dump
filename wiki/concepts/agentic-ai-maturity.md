# Agentic AI Maturity

Most companies (~90%) use AI in at least one function. A minority actually scale it. The gap isn't having AI — it's **orchestrating** it. This framework maps where organizations sit on the journey from "somebody spun up a chatbot" to "AI is a strategic capability."

## Market Projections

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Agent market growth | $7.8B → $52B by 2030 |
| Enterprise apps with agents (2026) | ~40% |
| Companies successfully scaling | Minority |

## The Three Levels

### 1. Experimentation
Individual teams build individual solutions. Local optimization. No coordination layer. This works for prototypes. It doesn't scale.

### 2. Systems Talking
Shared data foundations. Common platforms. **Where most enterprises are stuck.** They have the pieces but nobody's conducting the orchestra.

### 3. Strategic Integration
Coordination layers. Governance frameworks. Measurement systems. This is where competitive advantage lives.

## Quick Assessment

Ask teams what success looks like:

| Response Pattern | Gap Type |
|------------------|----------|
| Everyone gives different answers | **Coordination gap** |
| Can't connect metrics to business outcomes | **Orchestration gap** |
| Can't explain AI decisions' effects | **Governance/Auditability gap** |

## The OOAG Framework

**O**rchestration — **O**bservability — **A**uditability — **G**overnance

Winners in 2026 are defined by who masters OOAG, not who has the most AI. Having 17 AI systems that don't talk to each other is worse than having 3 that do. See [[ai-coordination-gap]] for the horror story version.
