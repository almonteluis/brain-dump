# Scaffolding

Scaffolding is the temporary help you give a kid so they can do something just beyond what they can pull off alone. The key word is *temporary*. If the support never fades, you haven't built competence — you've built dependency.

Wood, Bruner, and Ross coined the term in 1976, building on Vygotsky's [[zone-of-proximal-development]]. The ZPD identifies *where* the kid could go with help. Scaffolding is the *how* — the hints, prompts, models, and checklists that bridge the gap. And then, like ==actual scaffolding on a building, it comes down once the structure can stand on its own.==

## The Four Flavors

| Type | What it looks like | Example |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| **Conceptual** | Organizers that structure the problem | "Draw a number line before you start" |
| **Procedural** | Step-by-step hints that decrease over time | First: full walkthrough. Next: first step only. Then: just a nudge. |
| **Strategic** | Metacognitive prompts | "What have you tried so far?" |
| **Metacognitive** | Reflection and self-assessment | "How confident are you in that answer?" |

The design challenge: fading. Too fast and the kid crashes. Too slow and they stop trying — why struggle when the system will bail you out? [[knowledge-tracing]] is supposed to calibrate this, estimating when the kid is ready for less support. But if the KT estimate is wrong (and it often is at the edges), the scaffolding is wrong too.

## Why It's the Hard Problem in Ed-Tech

[[productive-struggle]] *requires* scaffolding — struggle without support is just frustration. But the whole point of productive struggle is that the kid *struggles*. Over-scaffold and you've shortcut the struggle, which means you've shortcut the learning. Under-scaffold and the kid gives up.

[[adaptive-feedback-education]] is scaffolding in AI form. [[insight-recall]] is metacognitive scaffolding — surfacing the kid's own past thinking at the moment they need it. [[tomlinson]]'s differentiated instruction is scaffolding at the classroom level: different supports for different readiness levels.

The thread worth pulling: every adaptive system eventually faces the moment where it has to *stop helping*. That's the design decision that separates a learning tool from a crutch. The system needs to be confident enough in its mastery estimate to pull the scaffold, and the kid needs to be ready enough not to collapse. Two different models, one moment of truth.
