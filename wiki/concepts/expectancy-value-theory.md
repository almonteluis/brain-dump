# Expectancy-Value Theory (EVT)

**Expectancy-Value Theory** (Eccles, Wigfield) posits that students' motivation to engage with academic tasks depends on two factors: their **expectancy of success** (belief they can succeed) and their **subjective task value** (how important, useful, or enjoyable they perceive the task to be). Task value itself is multidimensional, comprising intrinsic value, attainment value, utility value, and cost.

## EVT Constructs

| Construct | Definition | Example |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Expectancy of success** | Belief about likely success on the task | "I think I can do well in this math unit" |
| **Intrinsic value** | Enjoyment or interest in the task | "I actually like solving these kinds of problems" |
| **Attainment value** | Importance of doing well for personal identity | "Being good at math matters to who I am" |
| **Utility value** | Perceived usefulness for future goals | "This will help me get into a STEM program" |
| **Cost** | Perceived negative aspects (effort, time, anxiety) | "This takes too much time and stresses me out" |

## Profile Instability During Transitions

[[paper-evt-math-profiles-2024|Mayerhofer et al. (2024)]] measured all five constructs across the secondary-to-tertiary STEM transition:

- **Profile membership is unstable** — students shift between profiles across the transition
- **The transition is a critical window** — motivation profiles are most volatile here
- **Profiles predict cognitive effort and STEM engagement** at both time points
- **Cost matters** — it's not just about positive values; perceived cost differentiates profiles

## Integration with Other Frameworks

[[paper-evt-achievement-goals-2025|Xu (2025)]] integrated EVT with [[achievement-goal-theory]] and [[self-regulated-learning|self-regulation]] to identify six homework motivation profiles. The multi-theory approach captured more variance than any single framework, demonstrating that expectancy-value constructs combine with goals and self-regulation in heterogeneous ways.

## Connection to STEM Choices

[[paper-math-anxiety-stem-choices-2024|Cuder et al. (2024)]] found that math self-efficacy (closely related to expectancy) is the strongest pull toward STEM, while math anxiety (related to cost) pushes students away. The expectancy-value framework helps explain why some high-achieving students still avoid STEM — if perceived cost (anxiety, effort) outweighs value, even capable students disengage.

## Implications for Teach to One / New Classrooms

1. **Measure all five dimensions** — Don't just track "motivation" as a single score; assess expectancy, intrinsic value, attainment value, utility value, and cost separately
2. **Address cost explicitly** — Reduce perceived cost through scaffolded support, manageable time demands, and anxiety reduction
3. **Build utility value** — Help students see connections between math and their future goals
4. **Monitor profile transitions** — Students' motivation profiles shift; track them over time rather than assuming stability
5. **Target the secondary-to-tertiary window** — This is when profiles are most volatile and most amenable to intervention
6. **Combine with other frameworks** — EVT alone doesn't capture full motivation; integrate with goal theory and SRL
