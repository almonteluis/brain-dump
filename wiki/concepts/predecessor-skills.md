# Predecessor Skills

Predecessor skills are specific foundational competencies from earlier grades that directly predict success in target courses like Algebra 1. Not "all gaps." Not "everything the student missed." The *specific skills* that unlock the next level. The concept was quantified in [[Unlocking Algebra]] (TNTP & New Classrooms, 2025) and forms the empirical backbone of [[project-atlas|ATLAS]]'s sequencing engine.

## The Numbers

From the Unlocking Algebra analysis of 15,000+ students:

| Student Profile | Algebra 1 Success Rate |
|----------------|----------------------|
| Missing key predecessor skills | **13%** |
| Broad math knowledge but missing targeted predecessors | 31% |
| Broad knowledge + critical predecessor skills | **58%** |

13% to 58% based on whether you have the right foundational skills. Not more practice. Not more time. The *right* prerequisites.

## Why This Flips Traditional Remediation

Traditional remediation tries to fill **all** gaps. Every missing skill gets attention. The data says: don't do that. Instead:

1. **Identify the highest-leverage predecessors** for each learning objective
2. **Target only those** — not comprehensive gap-filling
3. **Pair foundational work** with grade-level content (don't hold students back)

This is the core insight behind ATLAS's sequencing: *success depends not on covering everything, but on mastering the right foundational skills.* [[knowledge-space-theory]] formalizes this. [[project-atlas|ATLAS]] operationalizes it.

## How ATLAS Uses This

The ATLAS sequencing engine maps predecessor skills through the [[knowledge-graph-education|knowledge graph]] and uses [[knowledge-tracing]] to identify which predecessors each student is missing. Then it prioritizes:

- Which predecessor gives the biggest lift for the next objective
- How to weave predecessor practice into current-grade content
- When to space predecessor review using [[spaced-retrieval-practice]]

## Connection to Three-Ring

In [[three-ring-mastery-system]], predecessor skill mastery fills the **outer ring** (new skills). Once mastered, the skill moves into the maintenance ring where [[insight-recall]] + [[spaced-retrieval-practice|spaced retrieval]] keep it active.

The open thread: predecessor skills are a static analysis — "here's what the graph says you need." But students aren't static. A kid who mastered fractions in September might have forgotten them by March. [[half-life-regression]] could predict *which* predecessors are decaying and need reinforcement, making predecessor targeting dynamic instead of one-time.
