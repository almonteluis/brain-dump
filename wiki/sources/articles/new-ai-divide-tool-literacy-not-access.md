# New AI Divide: Tool Literacy, Not Access

**Source:** raw/01_thinking/notes/New AI Divide Tool Literacy Not Access.md
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-09
**Type:** thinking note

## Summary

The new digital divide in education — gap between those who know how to use AI as a tool and those who don't know there's anything to know. Unlike the old divide (access to devices), this one is invisible and compounds over time.

## Key Claims

- The old digital divide was visible (have computer vs. don't)
- The new digital divide is invisible (can use AI effectively vs. don't know there's anything to know)
- You can have full ChatGPT access and still be on the wrong side of the gap
- This gap compounds because tool skills multiply

## The Old Digital Divide

**Then:** Access to computers and internet

**Solution:** Provide devices and connectivity

## The New Digital Divide

**Now:** Knowing how to use AI effectively

**Problem:** You can't see what you don't know exists

| Group | Relationship to AI |
|-------|-------------------|
| **Tool-literate** | AI as teammate, collaborator, multiplier |
| **Tool-illiterate** | Doesn't know there's anything to know |
| **Gap** | Growing, not shrinking |

## What the Tool-Illiterate Don't Know

- How to prompt effectively
- What to verify
- When AI lies
- What tasks are worth outsourcing
- How to integrate AI into workflows

## The Compounding Effect

This gap compounds because:

1. **Tool skills multiply** — Knowing how to use AI makes you more effective at learning how to use AI better
2. **Network effects** — Those who know share with others who know
3. **Resource advantage** — Well-resourced schools teach AI literacy; under-resourced schools don't

## The Connection to Management Skill

This parallels [[ai-as-management-skill]] — the people who integrate well with AI aren't just good at prompting, they're good at specific "senior-level" skills like context assembly and quality judgement.

**The new privilege:** Having someone teach you these skills.

## What This Means

If we don't address this:
- Best students will be fine (they have access to AI literacy education)
- Everyone else falls further behind
- The achievement gap becomes unbridgeable

**Solution:** Critical AI literacy must be part of core education, not a luxury.
