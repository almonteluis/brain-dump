To **research proven methods for math instruction and digital learning experiences**, focus on evidence-based strategies validated by educational research, particularly in STEM and technology-enhanced learning. Prioritize **peer-reviewed studies**, **randomized controlled trials (RCTs)**, and **meta-analyses** from reputable journals and organizations like the National Council of Teachers of Mathematics (NCTM) or the Institute of Education Sciences (IES). Since your team works with teachers and a web platform, integrate **formative assessment data**, **teacher feedback**, and **A/B testing** to validate what works in real classroom contexts.

### Prerequisites: Tools and Access
- Access to academic databases (e.g., Google Scholar, JSTOR, ERIC)
- Collaboration with educators or schools for field testing
	- This one's covered. We're currently working with North Dakota and a few other schools so that's covered 
- Analytics tools integrated into your web platform
	- This one is also covered. We used a heat map and we have our own custom analytics as well 
- Institutional access or open-access resources to bypass paywalls
	- As an organization I don't think we got access to this but I have my own methods 

### Step-by-step instructions
- Start by defining your research question using the **PICOT** or **SMART** framework (e.g., “How does personalized math content delivery via our platform impact student pacing in grades 6–8?”).
	- Look into the smart and picot framework
	- I'm more focused on it, like digital manipulative experiences to help push students' pace by revamping the platform's current practice experience. 
- Conduct a **literature review** using keywords like “effective math instruction,” “digital learning in mathematics,” “adaptive learning platforms,” and “student pacing in STEM.”
- Identify high-quality sources such as peer-reviewed journals (Educational Researcher, Journal for Research in Mathematics Education) and reports from Learning Policy Institute or NICHD.
- Focus on studies that use **experimental or quasi-experimental designs** to establish causality, especially those involving **blended learning**, **adaptive software**, or **formative feedback loops**.
- Extract key findings on effective practices: spaced practice, mastery learning, immediate feedback, gamification, and teacher-led digital integration.
- Use your web platform to **run small-scale A/B tests**—for example, compare two versions of a lesson to see which improves pacing or engagement.
- Collect both **quantitative data** (completion rates, time-on-task, assessment scores) and **qualitative feedback** (teacher interviews, student surveys) to triangulate results.
- Apply the **Six Star Science principles** or similar frameworks to ensure your interventions are research-based, student-centered, and iterative.
- Document and share findings with your team using collaborative tools (e.g., shared dashboards, reflection protocols).
- Iterate based on results—refine your platform features and instructional design continuously.
