# A.I. Should Elevate Your Thinking, Not Replace It

Blog post by Koshy John that sparked a major HN discussion (840+ points, 590+ comments, top ~1% of HN posts). The article argues that AI is creating a divide between engineers who use it to elevate their thinking and those who use it to avoid thinking entirely.

## Core Argument

Engineering is splitting into two groups:

1. **Elevators** — Use AI to remove drudgery, move faster, and spend time on what matters: framing problems, making tradeoffs, spotting risks, creating clarity, producing original insight.
2. **Outsourcers** — Paste prompts, collect polished output, present it as their own reasoning. Looks like productivity, but is a dead end.

## Critical Insights

### For Individual Engineers

> "Every time you substitute generated output for your own comprehension, you are skipping the exercises / reps that build judgment. You are trading long-term capability for short-term appearance."

The best engineers will:
- Let AI draft boilerplate, summarize docs, generate test scaffolding
- Ask sharper questions and define the real problem
- Generate new, high-value knowledge instead of rehashing existing knowledge
- Invest reclaimed time where it matters most

### For Early-Career Engineers

Foundational skills are built through friction, struggle, and getting things wrong. Using AI to remove all struggle from the learning loop hurts development. Someone who uses AI to answer every hard question may look efficient but is quietly failing to build the capabilities their future depends on.

### For Organizations

Strong engineering leadership in the AI era requires distinguishing polished output from real judgment. Leaders who cannot tell the difference may reward speed and fluency while missing signals of technical depth: originality, rigor, sound tradeoff analysis, and the ability to reason about unfamiliar problems.

> "If an organization allows low-understanding, high-fluency work to spread unchecked, it does not just lower the quality of individual output. It starts to degrade the knowledge environment itself."

## The Dividing Line

- If AI is helping you understand faster, think deeper, and operate at a higher level — it is making you more valuable.
- If AI is helping you avoid understanding, avoid struggle, and avoid ownership of reasoning — it is making you less valuable.

One path compounds. The other hollows you out.
