## One-line signal
A meta-analysis of 22 empirical studies (N = 5,232) finds generative AI has a positive overall effect on mathematics learning outcomes, with the strongest effects on conceptual understanding and reduced math anxiety.

## Core ideas
- Preregistered meta-analysis (INPLASY2025110051) reviewing 22 studies with 46 independent samples published between 2023 and 2025
- GenAI shows positive effects across multiple engagement dimensions: academic performance, math anxiety reduction, self-efficacy, and engagement
- Primary pupils reported reduced mathematics anxiety and increased classroom participation after using GenAI tools
- Effects on math anxiety and self-efficacy were particularly notable — GenAI may serve as a low-stakes practice environment
- Findings remain heterogeneous; moderator analysis needed to identify which GenAI features and contexts drive the strongest effects
- Gap identified: most studies focus on higher education; K-12 evidence is still emerging
- Concerns remain about over-reliance on AI and its impact on independent problem-solving skills

## Why this matters for the Three-Ring project
Provides empirical backing for using AI assistance in math education, particularly the finding that GenAI reduces math anxiety — a key barrier to engagement. However, the heterogeneity of results and concerns about over-reliance reinforce the need for Three-Ring to balance AI support with productive struggle, ensuring the tool builds confidence and competence rather than dependency.
