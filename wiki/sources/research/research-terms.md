Organized by how you’d actually use them — narrow enough to return signal, broad enough to surface adjacent fields you don’t know about yet.
Student pace — core concepts
	•	mastery-based progression
	•	self-paced learning (vs. competency-based — different traditions, both worth searching)
	•	learning velocity / learning gain
	•	pacing guides + adaptive curriculum
	•	time-on-task vs. mastery
	•	productive struggle (the term teachers actually use)
	•	desirable difficulties (cognitive science framing of the same idea)
Adaptive systems and student modeling
	•	knowledge tracing (umbrella term — DKT, BKT, SAKT are instances)
	•	[[intelligent tutoring systems]] (ITS) — older literature but foundational
	•	learner modeling
	•	open learner models (the subset where the model is shown back to the student — directly relevant to your rings)
	•	stealth assessment (Valerie Shute’s work — assessment without the student knowing they’re being assessed)
Teacher-facing
	•	teacher dashboards / learning analytics dashboards (LAD)
	•	orchestration tools (the literal term for what teachers do — there’s a whole subfield)
	•	co-orchestration / human-AI orchestration in classrooms
	•	formative assessment + data-informed instruction
	•	classroom analytics
	•	teacher noticing (teacher-cognition lit — how teachers actually decide who needs help)
	•	actionable analytics
The pace-teacher intersection
	•	differentiated instruction at scale
	•	small-group instruction + ability grouping (loaded term, search carefully)
	•	targeted intervention triggers
	•	early warning systems (predictive flags for struggling students)
	•	workload-aware teacher tools (newer subfield — tools designed around what teachers actually have time for)
Theoretical anchors (less for novelty, more for citation backbone)
	•	self-regulated learning (SRL) + metacognition
	•	cognitive load theory (Sweller)
	•	expertise reversal effect (why one-size-fits-all instruction breaks down)
	•	spacing effect + interleaving + retrieval practice (the “big three” of learning science)
Researchers worth following directly — their citation trails are gold
	•	Vincent Aleven, Ken Koedinger (CMU, Cognitive Tutor / Mathia)
	•	Ryan Baker (Penn, learning analytics)
	•	Neil Heffernan (ASSISTments — closest US-public-school analog to TTO)
	•	Kenneth Holstein (teacher-AI co-orchestration, Lumilo)
	•	Valerie Shute (stealth assessment)
	•	Inge Molenaar (adaptive learning + teacher dashboards, Netherlands)
	•	Justin Skycak (you have him already; check who he cites)
	•	Andy Matuschak (you have him; mine his “notes on” pages)
Conferences/venues — searching [topic] site:[venue] is a power move
	•	LAK (Learning Analytics & Knowledge)
	•	AIED (AI in Education)
	•	L@S (Learning at Scale, ACM)
	•	EDM (Educational Data Mining)
	•	ISLS / ICLS (Learning Sciences)
	•	arxiv cs.CY (Computers and Society)
Organizations that publish credible meta-research
	•	WestEd, RAND Education, MDRC (rigorous program evaluations)
	•	IES What Works Clearinghouse
	•	Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation reports
	•	Carnegie Learning, ASSISTments (own research arms)
Specific named systems worth knowing
	•	ASSISTments, Carnegie Learning Mathia, ALEKS, DreamBox, IXL, Khan Academy Khanmigo
	•	Lumilo (mixed-reality teacher orchestration), Luna (teacher dashboard), ClassroomMosaic
Search patterns that work better than just keywords
	•	[concept] middle school — filters out higher-ed-only results
	•	[concept] systematic review or [concept] meta-analysis — gets you the synthesis layer
	•	[concept] teacher perspective or teacher-facing [concept] — flips the lens
	•	failure modes [concept] or unintended consequences [concept] — the counterargument literature
	•	[two concepts] AND — bridge searches like “ZPD AND knowledge tracing” surface the integration papers
One meta-strategy: when you find a paper you like, scroll to its citations and look for the most-cited paper in its references. That’s usually the foundational text in the subfield — and the next paper you should read.
One filter worth applying: for ed-tech specifically, prioritize 2018+ unless you’re hunting foundational theory. The field moves fast and pre-2018 ITS literature often assumes constraints (no smartphones, no cloud LMS) that no longer apply.​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​
