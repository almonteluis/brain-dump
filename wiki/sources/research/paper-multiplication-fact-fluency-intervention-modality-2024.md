# Multiplication Fact Fluency: Modality Equivalence (Kromminga & Codding, 2024)

## Summary

Kromminga and Codding (2024) conducted a randomized controlled trial with 72 third graders comparing three intervention modalities for building multiplication fact fluency: reciprocal peer tutoring, iPad-delivered flashcards, and a combined (peer tutoring + iPad) condition. All three treatment groups improved significantly from pretest to posttest, with **no statistically significant differences between modalities on proximal (fluency) or distal measures**. The finding suggests that the active ingredient is repeated retrieval practice itself, not the delivery modality — digital tools work as well as human partners.

## Key Claims

1. **All three modalities produced significant fluency gains** — reciprocal peer tutoring, iPad flashcards, and combined all improved performance
2. **No significant differences between modalities** — the delivery mechanism (human vs. digital vs. combined) did not affect outcomes
3. **Retrieval practice is the active ingredient** — repeated recall of facts, regardless of how it's delivered, drives fluency gains
4. **Evidence-based interventions face a persistent translation gap** — effective fluency interventions exist but are often not implemented in schools
5. **Schools can choose modality based on context** — tech access, peer availability, and logistics can guide implementation without sacrificing efficacy
6. **Digital delivery is validated** — iPad flashcards match peer tutoring, supporting scalable digital fluency tools
