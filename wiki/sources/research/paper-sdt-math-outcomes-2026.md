# Self-Determination Theory and Math Outcomes (2026)

Paper applying [[self-determination-theory]] (Deci & Ryan) to math education outcomes. [[self-determination-theory|SDT]] says humans have three basic psychological needs: **autonomy** (I chose this), **competence** (I can do this), and **relatedness** (I belong here). When all three are met, intrinsic motivation kicks in. When they're thwarted, engagement drops and performance follows.

The connection to [[teach-to-one]]: the system is good at supporting competence ([[knowledge-tracing]] calibrates difficulty, [[master-based learning]] builds incremental wins). It's mediocre at autonomy (the playlist is generated, not chosen). It's weak at relatedness (no peer interaction, limited teacher relationship in the software layer). SDT would say TTO is operating with 1.5 out of 3 basic needs covered.

[[behavioral-nudges-education]] and gamification ([[three-ring-mastery-system]]) address engagement at the behavioral level. SDT goes deeper — it asks whether the engagement is *self-determined* or externally controlled. The difference matters for long-term persistence.
