# Math Anxiety and STEM Career Choices (2024)

Paper on how [[math-anxiety]] acts as a gatekeeper to STEM pathways. The mechanism: anxiety doesn't just feel bad, it *steals working memory*. A kid who knows the material but is anxious performs like a kid who doesn't know it. Over time, that experience compounds — the kid decides "I'm bad at math" and self-selects out of STEM courses, which closes off career paths.

Relevant to [[teach-to-one]] because anxiety management is an implicit design goal. If the system can keep kids in their [[zone-of-proximal-development]] and provide [[scaffolding]] that prevents catastrophic failure, it might reduce the anxiety spiral. But if the system *adds* pressure (timed assessments, visible rankings), it makes the problem worse.
