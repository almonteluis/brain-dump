# Metacognitive Instruction in Math Education: A Meta-Analysis (Hidayat et al. 2025)

## Summary

PRISMA-guided meta-analysis of studies examining the impact of metacognitive instruction on mathematics learning outcomes. Results show **large effect sizes** across all measured domains: math achievement (ES=1.11), metacognitive skills (ES=1.18), and other outcomes including problem-solving abilities and attitudes toward math (ES=1.27). Effects are robust across studies, suggesting metacognitive instruction is one of the strongest evidence-based interventions in education research. Benefits extend beyond performance to include improved problem-solving abilities and more positive attitudes toward mathematics.

## Key Claims

- Metacognitive instruction produces **large, robust effect sizes**: math achievement ES=1.11, metacognitive skills ES=1.18, other outcomes ES=1.27.
- These effect sizes are **among the largest in education research** — metacognitive instruction is not a marginal intervention but a high-impact one.
- Benefits extend **beyond performance** to include improved problem-solving abilities and more positive attitudes toward math.
- Effects are **robust across studies** — not dependent on a single methodology, age group, or context.
- The ES=1.27 for "other outcomes" (including attitudes) is particularly notable — metacognitive instruction doesn't just make students better at math, it changes how they *feel* about math.

## Why This Matters for Teach to One

This meta-analysis provides the strongest empirical mandate for embedding metacognitive instruction into Teach to One's adaptive learning platform. An effect size of ES=1.11 on math achievement means metacognitive instruction moves the needle more than most interventions TTO could invest in. The attitudinal findings (ES=1.27) are especially relevant — TTO's mission includes building math confidence, and metacognitive instruction directly improves attitudes. For [[just-in-time-adaptive-intervention]], this means scaffolds that prompt metacognition (reflection, planning, self-monitoring) should be treated as first-class instructional components, not optional add-ons. Combined with [[knowledge-tracing]], the system could identify when students are *not* engaging metacognitively and trigger targeted prompts — turning an already-effective intervention into a personalized one.
