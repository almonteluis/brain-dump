## One-line signal
A within-subjects study across nine introductory STEM courses found that spaced retrieval practice significantly improved retention in several courses (including precalculus), but effects were inconsistent — prompting the authors to ask "is the glass half full or half empty?"

## Core ideas
- Implemented spaced vs. massed retrieval practice on quizzes in nine different introductory STEM courses using a within-subjects design
- Based on prior precalculus/calculus findings (Hopkins et al., 2016; Lyle et al., 2020, 2022), they hypothesized spacing would boost retention in at least some courses
- Spacing improved performance in a precalculus course by 3–5% (~1/3 to 1/2 a letter grade), with even larger effects on long-term retention
- Results were inconsistent across the nine courses — some showed clear benefits, others did not, suggesting course-level factors moderate the spacing effect
- All cited spaced retrieval studies in math classrooms provided feedback to students, which may be an important enabling condition
- Concludes that spaced retrieval is promising but not a guaranteed win in every classroom context

## Why this matters for the Three-Ring project
Three-Ring should incorporate spaced retrieval into its practice engine, but this paper reminds us that the effect depends on implementation context. For middle/high school math, the precalculus findings are directly relevant — spacing at quiz level produces measurable retention gains even when effects are modest in some conditions.
