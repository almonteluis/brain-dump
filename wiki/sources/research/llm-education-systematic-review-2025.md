# LLMs in Education: A Systematic Review

## Summary

This systematic review analyzes 88 empirical studies published between November 2022 and March 2025, examining how large language models are being deployed across educational contexts. The review identifies four primary application areas: personalized learning pathways, automated assessment and feedback, classroom teaching support, and writing feedback/coaching. Across studies, LLMs demonstrate measurable improvements in learning outcomes, engagement, and efficiency. However, the review also highlights persistent risks including privacy concerns, algorithmic bias, hallucination, and the need for human oversight. The evidence base is growing rapidly but remains uneven in quality, with many studies lacking rigorous experimental designs.

## Key Claims

- LLMs are being deployed across four main educational use cases: personalized learning, assessment, classroom support, and writing feedback.
- Measurable improvements in learning outcomes are documented across multiple studies, though effect sizes vary.
- Privacy, bias, and hallucination remain significant and unresolved risks for educational deployment.
- Human oversight is consistently identified as necessary — fully autonomous AI tutoring lacks evidence of superiority.
- The evidence base is expanding rapidly but quality is uneven; more rigorous RCTs are needed.

## Why This Matters for Teach to One

This review provides the broadest evidence base for evaluating LLM integration into Teach to One's platform. The four application areas map directly to TON's needs: personalized learning pathways (already core to TON), automated assessment (enhancing [[knowledge-tracing]]), classroom support (for TON facilitators), and feedback generation. The consistent finding that human oversight is necessary validates TON's teacher-facilitated model. The risks section (privacy, bias, hallucination) should inform any AI feature development — particularly for K-12 audiences where data governance is critical. See also [[its-llm-architecture]] for patterns that address these risks through modular design.
