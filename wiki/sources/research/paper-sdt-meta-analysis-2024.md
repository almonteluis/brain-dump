# Wang et al. (2024) — Meta-Analysis of SDT-Based Educational Interventions

**Source:** [Learning and Motivation](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/2024_WangWangEtAl_MetaEdu.pdf)
**Authors:** Wang, Y., Wang, H., Wang, S., Wind, S.A., & Gill, C.

## Summary

Meta-analysis of 36 [[self-determination-theory|SDT]]-based educational interventions found consistent improvements in students' intrinsic motivation, perceived autonomy, and perceived competence. Autonomy support emerged as the key active ingredient — interventions that specifically target autonomy support show the strongest effects on intrinsic motivation. More autonomous forms of motivation consistently predict better learning outcomes. Expands scope beyond prior meta-analyses (Lazowski & Hulleman 2016; Reeve & Cheon 2021) by focusing specifically on [[self-determination-theory|SDT]]-grounded classroom interventions.

## Key Claims

- [[self-determination-theory|SDT]]-based interventions significantly improve intrinsic motivation, autonomy, and competence
- Autonomy support is the key active ingredient for fostering intrinsic motivation
- More autonomous forms of motivation predict better learning outcomes
- Effects are consistent across educational contexts
- 36 studies provide a robust evidence base for SDT as an intervention framework
