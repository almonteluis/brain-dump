# Shank, Tang & Morris (2024) — [[self-determination-theory|SDT]]-Based Online Mathematics Unit

**Source:** [Journal of Interactive Media in Education](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-024-10410-9)
**Authors:** Shank, E.R., Tang, H., & Morris, W.

## Summary

Implemented an [[self-determination-theory|SDT]]-based online mathematics unit using action research methodology. Post-intervention, students showed significant increases in competence and relatedness, but autonomy gains were less evident quantitatively (though student interviews suggested some perceived autonomy benefits). Demonstrates that SDT-based course design is feasible and effective in online secondary math contexts, but autonomy support may require more intentional scaffolding in digital environments. Online math students particularly struggle with self-regulation and self-efficacy, making SDT-informed design especially relevant.

## Key Claims

- SDT-based online math design significantly improves competence and relatedness
- Autonomy is harder to achieve in digital environments and requires intentional scaffolding
- Online math students particularly struggle with self-regulation and self-efficacy
- SDT principles are transferable to digital math learning contexts
- Mixed-methods (quantitative + interview) reveal nuances that pure surveys miss

## Entities Mentioned

- SDT three basic needs: autonomy, competence, relatedness
- Online mathematics unit (action research design)
- Competence and relatedness (improved)
- Autonomy (less improvement quantitatively)
- Self-regulation and self-efficacy as challenges in online math
- Student interviews as qualitative complement
