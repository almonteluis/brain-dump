# Metacognition, Computational Thinking, and Mathematical Modeling (Zhao et al. 2024)

## Summary

Large-scale study (N=661 high school students) examining how metacognition relates to mathematical modeling skills. Metacognition predicts mathematical modeling ability both directly and indirectly through computational thinking — specifically its **critical thinking** sub-dimension. This establishes a causal chain: metacognition → critical thinking → mathematical modeling. Computational thinking acts as a bridge between metacognitive awareness and applied mathematical problem-solving.

## Key Claims

- Metacognition is a **significant direct predictor** of mathematical modeling skills.
- The relationship is also **mediated by computational thinking**, specifically its critical thinking dimension.
- **Causal chain**: metacognition → critical thinking → mathematical modeling performance.
- Computational thinking bridges metacognitive awareness and applied problem-solving — it's the mechanism through which metacognitive monitoring translates into better modeling outcomes.
- Sample size (N=661) provides strong statistical power for mediation analysis.

## Why This Matters for Teach to One

Teach to One's personalized learning pathways implicitly ask students to engage in mathematical modeling — translating real-world scenarios into mathematical structures. This paper identifies **metacognition and critical thinking** as the upstream levers that drive modeling performance. If TTO can scaffold metacognitive awareness (e.g., through reflective prompts before/during multi-step problems), it may strengthen the critical thinking pipeline that feeds into better modeling. This connects to [[just-in-time-adaptive-intervention]] — interventions timed to prompt metacognitive activity could amplify downstream modeling gains. The mediation finding also suggests that [[productive-struggle]] on complex problems may be most effective when paired with metacognitive scaffolding.
