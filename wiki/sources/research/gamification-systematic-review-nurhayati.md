# Gamification in School Education: Systematic Review (Nurhayati 2025)

**Source:** [doi.org/10.35445/alishlah.v17i2.6516](https://doi.org/10.35445/alishlah.v17i2.6516) — Nurhayati Nurhayati, Fathurrohman Fathurrohman, Universitas Negeri Yogyakarta
**Date ingested:** 2026-05-09
**Type:** Systematic literature review (PRISMA)
**Journal:** AL-ISHLAH: Jurnal Pendidikan, Vol 17 No 2 (2025)

## Summary

Systematic literature review assessing effects of [[gamification]] on student motivation and learning outcomes across educational levels. Followed PRISMA framework. Initial pool: 278 articles from Scopus + ERIC. After screening + quality assessment: **21 peer-reviewed studies** (2021–2024) included, quality scores 60%–100% (avg 82%).

## Key Findings

- Gamification **positively influences student motivation** and **often enhances academic performance**
- Strongest in **primary and secondary education**
- Effectiveness **varies** by subject matter, educational stage, technological infrastructure
- Context-dependent — not a universal panacea

## Most Effective Game Elements (Identified)

| Element | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Points | Numerical progress signal |
| Badges | Achievement signals |
| Leaderboards | Competitive ranking |
| Progress tracking | Visual completion cues |
| Real-time feedback | Immediate response loop |
| Collaboration | Social engagement |
| Real-world applications | Authentic transfer contexts |
| Autonomy support | Self-directed pacing |
Recently I've been hearing pushback on gamification and applications as of late. Badges, points, leaderboards are being overused and kind of creating the opposite effects from what the application intended to do.

One example is one app that was primarily focused on building habits. Gamifying the app experience made it like an RPG-like game. Users were more focused on making sure that their little avatars were alive and healthy and the crops were growing. If you missed a day of doing a certain habit, those avatars would die and stuff. It's a bit extreme but it was reported that users only logged on and just did their daily check-ins to keep their streaks alive. It had a negative effect where it was producing anxiety not excitement. They would just come off of the app, do the bare minimum to get their points to keep their streak alive and keep them on the leaderboard, and move off of it.

Gamification for an ad tech company product is not the best idea for now 
## Boundary Conditions / Caveats

- Concerns about **deep learning** vs surface engagement
- **Digital access disparities** between institutions limit equitable deployment
- "Dark side of narrow gamification" cited (Kwon & Özpolat 2021) — overly assessment-gamified contexts can negatively impact perceptions and content knowledge

## Practical Implications

> To maximize potential, educators must adopt balanced approaches that consider student needs, curricular goals, and available resources. Gamification can significantly enhance student learning and motivation when implemented strategically and supported by robust pedagogical and technological frameworks.
