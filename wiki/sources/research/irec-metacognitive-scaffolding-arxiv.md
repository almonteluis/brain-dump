# Insight Recall for Metacognitive Scaffolding (Hou & Tan, 2025)

This is the [[xuefei-hou]] & [[xizhao-tan]] paper that proposes [[insight-recall]] as an alternative to traditional [[spaced-repetition]]. The core idea: learning systems already capture student insights (reflections, strategies, "aha" moments) — and then throw them away. IRec says: store them, index them by context, and surface them at the moment the student encounters a similar situation. Not on a timer. When the *situation* triggers it.

This addresses the [[encoding-specificity-principle]] problem directly: de-contextualized flashcard review produces weak retrieval because the cue doesn't match the encoding. IRec surfaces the insight *in context*, so the retrieval cue matches the original encoding situation.

Directly relevant to the [[three-ring-mastery-system]] maintenance ring design and [[project-atlas]]. The open question: what happens when the surfaced insight was wrong? Surfacing a misconception at the wrong moment could reinforce it.
