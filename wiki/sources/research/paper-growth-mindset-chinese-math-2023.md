# Growth Mindset Interventions in Chinese Math Classrooms (2023)

Cross-cultural test of growth mindset interventions in Chinese math education. Dweck's framework ("intelligence can be developed") has mixed evidence in Western contexts and hadn't been robustly tested in East Asian educational cultures where effort is already strongly valorized. The question: does a growth mindset intervention add anything when the culture already emphasizes effort?

The answer matters for [[teach-to-one]] because mindset messaging is low-hanging fruit in adaptive feedback — "you got better at this!" is easy to generate. But if the intervention effect is small or culturally specific, it's not worth building product around. The deeper connection: [[math-anxiety]] is partly a fixed-mindset problem ("I'm just not a math person"). [[achievement-goal-theory]] says mastery goals (growth mindset-adjacent) predict better persistence than performance goals.
