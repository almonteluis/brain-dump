# Xu & Dieckmann (2025) — Mathematical Mindset, Growth Mindset, and Self-Efficacy Are Distinct

**Source:** [Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1598817/full)
**Authors:** Xi Yu Xu, Joanne A. Dieckmann

## Summary

Argues that mathematical mindset (domain-specific beliefs about math ability), growth mindset (general beliefs about intelligence malleability), and self-efficacy (confidence in performing specific tasks) are often conflated but are empirically distinct constructs. Each influences math achievement through different pathways: cognitive flexibility, problem-solving strategy use, and motivational persistence respectively. Uses a neuroplasticity framework to design targeted interventions for each construct separately. Builds on YouCubed (Jo Boaler, Stanford) research program.

## Key Claims

- Mathematical mindset, growth mindset, and self-efficacy are empirically distinct constructs
- Each construct has a unique pathway to math achievement (cognitive flexibility, strategy use, motivational persistence)
- Treating these constructs as interchangeable leads to muddled intervention outcomes
- Specificity in measurement and intervention design matters
- Neuroplasticity-based interventions can target each construct separately
