## One-line signal
Proposes skill trees as a formal structure for organizing competency-based courses, enabling automated coaching algorithms and better management of digitalized educational resources by explicitly modeling skill prerequisites and dependencies.

## Core ideas
- Competency-based education shifts focus from content (what is taught) to skills (what students can do)
- Skill trees formally represent skill prerequisites and dependencies — a student must master prerequisite skills before advancing
- Enables automated coaching: algorithms can reason about student skill levels, detect deficiencies, and recommend paths
- Addresses the challenge of properly structuring courses for automation at scale
- Discusses properties of valid skill trees: acyclicity, prerequisite coverage, coherence
- Differentiates skill trees from traditional curriculum graphs by focusing on demonstrable competencies
- Published at Koli Calling '25 (CS education conference)

## Why this matters for the Three-Ring project
Skill trees are a natural fit for Three-Ring's progression system — students can visualize their math skill mastery as a tree they're climbing, with clear prerequisites and unlockable abilities. This gamification-like structure is inherently motivating and gives students a sense of progress and agency. The formal properties discussed (acyclicity, prerequisite coverage) provide design constraints for building a valid math skill tree.
