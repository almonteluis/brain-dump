# High Schoolers as Effective Fluency Interventionists (Powell et al., 2024)

## Summary

Powell, Rist, Stein, Banks, Villanueva, Alwadi, Dupree, Frau-Canabal, and Patel (2024) tested whether high school students could deliver elementary math fact fluency interventions as effectively as professional school-based interventionists. Using a 2 × 2 mixed factorial ANOVA comparing pre- to post-test growth across interventionist education levels, they found **significant growth in math fact fluency from pre- to post-test with no significant differences by interventionist education level**. High schoolers performed comparably to professionals, validating cross-age peer tutoring as a scalable delivery model.

## Key Claims

1. **High schoolers deliver interventions as effectively as professionals** — no significant difference in student outcomes between high-school and professional interventionists
2. **The fluency intervention itself produces measurable gains** — significant pre-to-post growth regardless of who delivers it
3. **Cross-age peer tutoring is viable and scalable** — schools can expand intervention capacity by recruiting and training high school students
4. **Social motivation may be an underexplored lever** — older peers may provide relational benefits (role modeling, approachability) that professionals cannot
5. **Future research needed** — larger samples, waitlist controls, and comparison of intervention modalities (flashcard drill, cover/copy/compare) recommended
