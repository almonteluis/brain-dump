## One-line signal
Students can meaningfully evaluate their teachers' support for productive struggle, and their perceptions reveal that emotional scaffolding is as important as cognitive scaffolding during difficult math tasks.

## Core ideas
- Mixed-methods study examining how students perceive and evaluate teacher support for productive struggle in mathematics
- Productive struggle is identified by NCTM as a key effective teaching practice, involving the emotional dimension of math learning
- Students distinguished between teachers who truly supported productive struggle and those who simply gave answers or withdrew support
- Key teacher behaviors students valued: patience, encouragement without giving away answers, providing hints rather than solutions, and normalizing difficulty
- Students reported higher motivation and deeper understanding when teachers maintained the struggle rather than rescuing them
- The emotional aspect of productive struggle (feeling safe to fail, managing frustration) was highlighted as critical

## Why this matters for the Three-Ring project
Directly informs how Three-Ring should design its AI tutoring interactions. Instead of immediately providing answers, the app should scaffold with hints and encouragement, normalize difficulty, and help students manage frustration — mirroring what students report as effective teacher behavior. The finding that emotional support matters as much as cognitive support suggests the app should include motivational messaging alongside academic help.
