# Nudge Megastudy — Teacher Email Nudges and Student Math Progress (2025)

**Authors:** Angela L. Duckworth, Ahra Ko, Katherine L. Milkman, Joseph S. Kay, Eugen Dimant, Dena M. Gromet, Aden Halpern, Youngwoo Jung, Madeline K. Paxson, Ramon A. Silvera Zumaran, Ron Berman, Ilana Brody, Colin F. Camerer, D. Bulte
**URL:** https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2418616122
**Cross-links:** [[paper-streaks-math-engagement]], [[paper-nudge-meta-synthesis-2025]]

## Summary

This megastudy — one of the largest behavioral experiments in education — tested email-based nudges sent to approximately 10,000 teachers of roughly 3 million elementary students using [[Zearn]] Math. On average, teacher nudges boosted student math progress by 1.89%. The critical finding was that **personalized, data-driven nudges** significantly outperformed generic reminders, confirming that specificity and relevance drive nudge effectiveness. Effective nudges leveraged **loss aversion** (highlighting students falling behind), **social norms** (comparing class progress to peers), and **actionable student progress data** (giving teachers concrete next steps). The scale of this study provides strong external validity for applying behavioral nudges within adaptive math platforms, with direct implications for how [[Teach to One]] and [[New Classrooms]] communicate student progress information to teachers and school leaders.

## Key Claims

- Teacher-facing email nudges produced a **statistically significant 1.89% increase** in student math progress at massive scale (N ≈ 3 million students).
- **Personalized, data-driven nudges** substantially outperformed generic reminder emails — content specificity matters.
- Three psychological mechanisms drove effectiveness: **loss aversion** (highlighting at-risk students), **social norms** (peer benchmarking), and **actionable data** (specific student-level recommendations).
- Nudge effects varied across teacher subpopulations, suggesting **heterogeneous treatment effects** — not all teachers respond equally.
- The megastudy design (multiple nudges tested simultaneously) enables comparison across strategies rather than single-arm evaluation.
- Results reinforce that **teacher behavior is a leverable pathway** to improving student outcomes, complementing direct student-facing interventions like streaks ([[paper-streaks-math-engagement]]).
