## One-line signal
AI in education risks undermining productive struggle — the effortful cognitive work essential for deep learning — and educators must intentionally design AI tools that support rather than shortcut the struggle.

## Core ideas
- Explores when AI-provided ease promotes learning vs. when it becomes a harmful shortcut
- Examines AI's influence on students' memory, attention, motivation, and self-regulation
- Productive struggle is defined as the effortful process of grappling with challenging material that leads to deeper understanding
- AI tools that immediately provide answers or over-scaffold can reduce cognitive engagement and weaken learning
- Recommends designing AI tools that provide "just-in-time" support while preserving the learner's cognitive effort
- Highlights the tension between AI's efficiency and the educational value of difficulty and failure
- Calls for more research on how different AI interaction patterns affect long-term learning outcomes and youth development

## Why this matters for the Three-Ring project
A critical design constraint for Three-Ring: the app must enhance productive struggle rather than eliminate it. This means designing AI assistance that hints, prompts, and encourages without giving answers outright. The report's warning about over-scaffolding should guide Three-Ring's difficulty calibration — problems should remain challenging even with AI support available.
