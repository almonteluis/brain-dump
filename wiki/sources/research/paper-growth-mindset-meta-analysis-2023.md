# Macnamara & Burgoyne (2023) — Growth Mindset Interventions: Near-Zero Effects

**Source:** [Psychological Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36326645/)
**Authors:** Brooke N. Macnamara, Samantha Burgoyne

## Summary

Comprehensive meta-analysis of the growth mindset intervention evidence base found that effects shrink to near zero when rigorous design standards are applied: active control conditions, preregistered moderators, and baseline equivalence. Publication bias inflates reported effect sizes. No subgroup among 11 preregistered moderators showed a robust, practically significant effect. The paper sparked significant controversy with published commentaries and a rejoinder from the authors.

## Key Claims

- Growth mindset intervention effects are near zero when design flaws are controlled
- Publication bias meaningfully inflates reported effect sizes
- Active control conditions (vs. no-treatment controls) eliminate most effects
- No subgroup shows a robust, practically significant effect across 11 preregistered moderators
- Unpublished/null findings exist but are underrepresented in the literature
- The controversy itself (Burnette et al., Tipton et al. commentaries) indicates the debate is not settled but the rigorous evidence is weak

## Entities Mentioned

- Growth mindset interventions as a category
- Meta-analytic methods: publication bias correction, active controls, baseline equivalence
- 11 preregistered moderators
