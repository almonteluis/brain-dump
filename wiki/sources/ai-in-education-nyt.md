# How Teachers and Students Feel About AI

**Source:** raw/00_inbox/Links/How teachers and students feel about AI.md
**Original:** NYTimes - Natasha Singer, Aug 24, 2023
**URL:** https://www.nytimes.com/2023/08/24/technology/how-teachers-and-students-feel-about-ai.html
**Date ingested:** 2026-04-09
**Type:** News article

## Summary

NYT article on the shift from AI bans to embracing AI tools in education. Covers teacher/student perspectives, effective use cases, detection challenges, and the new equity gap forming around AI literacy.

## Key Claims

- Schools initially blocked ChatGPT (February) then unblocked (March) and now embrace it
- AI great for: quiz variations, instruction clarity, brainstorming
- AI poor for: essay evaluation, critical analysis
- AI detection is impossible — "A.I. use is undetectable"
- New digital divide: those who know how to use AI vs those who don't

## Teacher Use Cases

| Use Case                  | Effectiveness |
| ------------------------- | ------------- |
| Quiz variations           | Excellent     |
| Instruction clarity check | Excellent     |
| Brainstorming activities  | Excellent     |
| Essay evaluation          | Poor          |

## Student Perspectives

**Benefits:**
- Personalized tutoring ("never gets tired")
- Simplified concepts
- Endless examples and outlines
- Time savings

**Concerns:**
- "Industrialized plagiarism"
- Loss of "AHA!" discovery moments
- Lazy thinking — not engaging deeply with texts

## Critical Quotes

> "The short answer is no. The long answer is A.I. use is undetectable."
> — Ethan Mollick, Wharton School

> "As a teacher, if I'm asking questions that are easily answered by A.I., am I asking the best questions?"
> — Jennifer Parnell, history teacher

> "I foresee an ever yawning gap between the privileged and everyone else, between those who know how to use A.I. as a tool and those who don't know that there is anything to know."
> — Ricardo Galliano Court, Northwestern University

## Key Insights

1. Banning failed — schools now embracing AI
2. AI great for ideation, poor for evaluation
3. Literacy over access — teaching critical usage matters most
4. Detection impossible — adapt rather than catch
5. Assessment must evolve — rethink questions AI can answer
6. Equity risk — new divide emerging

## What Students Should Demand

- Clarity on what's allowed
- Can AI help generate ideas?
- Can AI provide outlines to work on?
- Can AI give feedback?
- Can AI be a teammate?
- Can AI explain why I got something wrong?
