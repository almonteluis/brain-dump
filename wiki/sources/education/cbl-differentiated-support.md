# CBL Differentiated Support — Source Summary

> Research synthesis on how to provide differentiated, equitable, and objectively-grounded support within a competency-based learning platform. Part of [[operation-endgame]] Pillar 3.

## Overview

This source addresses a core challenge of CBL: once you know where each student is and where they need to go, **how do you get them there?** The right support must be delivered at the right time, for the right student, without introducing bias. The note synthesizes five foundational frameworks and connects them to existing vault research.

## Foundational Frameworks Covered

1. **[[differentiated-instruction]]** — [[tomlinson]] (1999): differentiate by readiness, interest, learning profile across content, process, product, environment. In CBL, differentiation is driven by demonstrated competency rather than age/grade.
2. **[[scaffolding]]** — [[vygotsky]]'s ZPD → Wood, Bruner & Ross (1976) original scaffolding concept → Van de Pol et al. (2010) review → Belland et al. (2017) meta-analysis (g ≈ 0.50). Four types: conceptual, strategic, metacognitive, motivational.
3. **AI Tutoring** — Khanmigo (Slijepcevic & Yaylali 2025), Brookings (2024), Chen et al. (2025) Nature systematic review. Lower-performing students benefit most; ITS should be deployed with differentiated instructional designs.
4. **[[culturally-responsive-pedagogy]]** — [[ladson-billings]] (1995) three pillars. Competency Collaborative (2023) shows CBE benefits marginalized students. Aurora Institute equity strategies.
5. **Real-Time Intervention** — Martin et al. (2020): AI-enhanced learning analytics for at-risk identification and timely intervention.

## Key Claims

- Differentiation in CBL should be driven by demonstrated competency data, not teacher assumptions or demographic proxies
- Autonomy support is the critical active ingredient in differentiated interventions (Wang et al. 2024 meta-analysis)
- Scaffolding has consistently strong effects (g ≈ 0.50) but must be contingent, faded, and aimed at transfer
- The human-AI copilot model (AI augmenting teachers) has the highest near-term impact for differentiated support
- Behavioral nudges are a scalable, low-cost differentiated support layer
- Support must be affective (addressing math anxiety, providing emotional scaffolding), not just cognitive
- Culturally sustaining pedagogy — multiple pathways, flexible pacing, transparent criteria — benefits marginalized students
