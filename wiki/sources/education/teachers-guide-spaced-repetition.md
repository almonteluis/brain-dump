# A Teacher's Guide to Spaced Repetition

**Source:** [thirdspacelearning.com](https://thirdspacelearning.com/us/blog/spaced-repetition/) — Zoe Benjamin (revised by Christi Kulesza), 2024-10-02
**Date ingested:** 2026-05-03
**Type:** Pedagogical guide

## Summary

Pedagogical playbook for applying spaced repetition in K-12 classrooms, especially mathematics. Covers Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve, the Leitner system for paper flashcards, repetition schedules at 1/3/6/10/14/18 week intervals, and 7 practical classroom techniques. Companion app angle plugs Skye AI math tutor (Third Space Learning).

## Forgetting Curve + Spacing Effect

Ebbinghaus (late 19th century): newly learned info decays quickly, then more slowly. Without reinforcement, most newly-learned content is forgotten. **Spaced practice reduces decay rate** with each subsequent review session.

Rawson et al. (2013): students reviewing vocabulary across multiple sessions outperformed students who studied for the same total time in a single session — both immediately after and four weeks later.

## Spaced Repetition Techniques

- **Active recall** — retrieving info from long-term memory without cues. Flashcards only count if learner answers before flipping.
- **Leitner system** — flashcards organized into compartments by retention strength. Correct → next compartment (longer interval). Incorrect → first compartment.
- **Repetition schedules** — short intervals after initial learning, then expand. **SuperMemo** (Piotr Wozniak) calculates optimal intervals based on performance + complexity.

## Classroom Schedule

Insert review at: **1, 3, 6, 10, 14, 18 weeks** post-initial-teaching.

Spaced repetition column in scope and sequence prompts teachers to revisit prior material via:
- Homework
- Low-stakes quiz
- Interleaving with new topic

**Personalized schedules are time-consuming**. Leitner system (student-managed) is the practical compromise. Recommended apps: Anki, Quizlet, Mathspace.

## 7 Classroom Techniques

1. **Flashcards + Leitner** — particularly for language learning or terminology-heavy subjects
2. **Low-stakes quizzes** — 5min weekly. No grade weight. Multiple formats: single-detailed, 5 short-answer mini-whiteboards, spelling/times tables, definitions
3. **Lesson starters** — short entry activity. Brain dump (write everything you remember), past paper question, or low-stakes quiz
4. **Homework exercises** — half of homework dedicated to prior learning. Review 2-3 previous topics weekly
5. **Regular testing** — interleave revision topics into tests, even if unrelated to recently taught content
6. **Interleaving** — combine 2+ topics into single questions. Adds desirable difficulty. Example: fractions + decimals + percentages mixed instead of separate weeks
7. **Scheme-of-work planning** — adapt curriculum to include reminder of which topics to review each week

## 8 Math Interleaving Examples

1. Lengths as fractions/surds/decimals when finding area + perimeter
2. Mix cm + mm in geometry questions (forces unit conversion)
3. Link nth term with plotting straight-line equation
4. Percentage answers to multiple degrees of accuracy
5. Calculate percentages + fractions from bar charts
6. Time as fractions of an hour → convert to minutes
7. BODMAS reminders when finding area of trapezium
8. Test scores as fractions → convert to percentage

## Key Claims

- Spaced repetition combats Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve via repeated retrieval
- Classroom-friendly schedule: 1/3/6/10/14/18 weeks post-initial-teaching
- Spiral curriculum = built-in spaced repetition (revisit topics with increasing complexity)
- Personalized schedules are impractical at scale — Leitner-style student-managed systems are the compromise
- Interleaving across related topics adds **desirable difficulty** that improves long-term retention
