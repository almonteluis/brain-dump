determining what students should learn: the personalized roadmap. Each student receives a uniquely sequenced progression of skills, not a single lockstep curriculum, but a personalized pathway tailored to their current proficiencies, learning cadence, and teacher-set instructions. Today, the Teach to Learn roadmap operates on a robust set of heuristic rules, drawing on more than a decade of classroom knowledge about how students learn math. These rules analyze assessment data, identify readiness patterns, consider classroom priorities, and recommend the next skills. This approach has been proven effective within its design constraints.

AI opportunity: Machine learning models can take this rule set much further with greater precision. Informative assessment outcomes, engagement patterns, and questions like: How long does a student work? Where do they persist and where do they disengage? Time dynamics over weeks, learning velocity, forgetting curves from spaced repetition data, and even semantic features of the content topics. Recommendations, informed by the models, can directly account for teacher-set priorities.

Critically, the unlock in algebra research is a key design principle: the system’s sequence and engine should identify which predecessors’ skills are the highest leverage for each student, rather than attempting to fill every gap comprehensively. Models trained on years of learning and interaction data can predict which elements yield the strongest outcomes and which predecessors’ skills unlock the most future learning for each individual student.
