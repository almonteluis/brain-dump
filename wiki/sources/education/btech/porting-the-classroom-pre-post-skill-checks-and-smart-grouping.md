I don’t want to reinvent the wheel. There’s already a lot of good stuff in place, and I’d rather start by mapping what’s currently being used — even if just on paper — and then figure out what’s worth building on digitally.
The framing I keep coming back to is: take a classroom experience that already works well and find the online equivalent. Not replace it — port the best parts of it. What makes group activities and discussions effective in a physical classroom, and how do we translate that into a digital format?
One idea that came up during a hackathon was a pre-skill check. The teacher logs in, the whole class takes a short quiz, and once everyone submits, the results are processed and surfaced on the teacher’s dashboard — synced, not real-time, since the data is essentially a snapshot once the quiz closes. From there, the teacher can see how the class responded and use that to form discussion groups.
Where it gets interesting is the grouping algorithm. There are at least two valid approaches that produce very different dynamics:
Similar struggle — group kids who got stuck on the same things. The goal is collaborative sense-making, surfacing shared misconceptions through discussion.
Complementary understanding — pair kids who got it with kids who didn’t. Peer teaching, which research shows benefits both sides.
The platform doesn’t have to pick one. It could surface both options and let the teacher decide based on what they’re going for that day.
The natural extension of this is a pre and post-skill check — run the quiz before instruction and again after, so you have a clear before-and-after on how the class moved. That gives the teacher something concrete to reflect on and gives the platform data to track real learning shifts.
This isn’t a built-in feature yet — it’s more a direction worth exploring and bringing to teachers to see what resonates.
