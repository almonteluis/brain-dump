# Source: CBL — Assessing Demonstrations

> Research synthesis on competency-based assessment for [[Operation Endgame]] Pillar 1.

## Summary

This source establishes how [[competency based learning]] platforms should assess demonstrated mastery rather than seat-time or compliance. It synthesizes three research streams — [[performance-based-assessment]] (Stiggins, Tobón, Stefanakis), [[formative-assessment]] (Black & Wiliam, Hattie), and equity-focused assessment (Aurora Institute) — and maps them onto existing vault research: [[skill-tree-competency-structure]], [[three-ring-mastery-system]], [[insight-recall]], and [[reverse-scoring]].

## Key Claims

1. Traditional assessment measures seat-time and recall; CBL assessment measures **what students can actually do** through multiple forms of evidence.
2. Formative assessment (d = 0.90 per Hattie) is among the highest-impact interventions; CBL leverages it by replacing high-stakes summative events with continuous low-stakes demonstration.
3. [[skill-tree-competency-structure]] DAGs enable **automated gap detection** — given a target competency, identify unmastered prerequisites.
4. [[three-ring-mastery-system]] stages (New → Practice → Mastered → Maintained) serve as **assessment milestones**, with skills-mastered-per-week as the primary metric aligned with Hattie's mastery learning evidence (d = 0.57).
5. [[insight-recall]] extends assessment beyond content mastery to **metacognitive demonstration** — from reviewing content to reactivating strategy.
6. [[reverse-scoring]] ensures psychometric validity for affective measures (e.g., self-efficacy in BrainBreak) alongside academic competencies.
7. Assessment in CBL should be transparent, equity-focused, and support multiple pathways to demonstrate mastery (Aurora Institute, 2020; Competency Collaborative, 2023).
