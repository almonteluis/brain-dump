I read a paper about IREC where they are doing something where it is metacognition. I still can't pronounce that word fully but it's a process of thinking of your own thinking. If a student is doing a skill and within that prerequisite skill tree they already probably have mastered a certain skill before, or past that certain skill, so right before that they do a skill challenge or attempt a new skill, we should surface previous skills. The paper was pretty much saying surface insights from other workings, from other math problems that are not related to the particular skill. It's like we could surface their insights on how they figure out how to solve an equation and we should surface that when they're struggling on a related one.

The only thing that's fuzzy for me is the implementation part. We don't have insights throughout the entire platform. We do have exit slips but that's only for my Miami not everyone within our program. I do like this. It's a good feature but I do want to explore other papers and other ways to improve student motivation and pace. I think another thing I do want to research is persistence.

I was reading an article the other day about when a teacher assigns work to the entire class. It was painting this scenario where everybody was ready, everybody was motivated to start working on a new skill. Once they start working on it, the article was describing the typical scenario of, like, "Oh everybody's excited now. They got dependent papers, they are working on the math problems by themselves, and the chatter is now decreasing." Now you see some kids like fidgeting, looking around to see what other people are doing. Some kids started writing and then they hit a roadblock and then it stopped and they don't know how to persist when they are confused, lost, or unsure what the next steps are.

I think we can just build in a mechanism to reward persistence and maybe guide the user without giving them the answer. This is pretty much like a scaffolding issue: what would be the best way to guide the user without giving them too many hints to let them solve it? These are just thoughts rambling in my head.
