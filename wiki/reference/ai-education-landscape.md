# AI Education Landscape

Source: Natasha Singer, NYTimes, Aug 24 2023.

## The Shift: Bans to Embrace

Walla Walla High School went from blocking ChatGPT (February) to unblocking it (March) to integrating it into curriculum (by end of year). Same arc across districts nationwide — panic, then adaptation. The bans never worked anyway.

## Teacher Use Cases That Work

| Use Case | Effectiveness |
|----------|---------------|
| **Quiz variations** | Excellent — generates multiple versions fast |
| **Instruction clarity check** | Excellent — catches ambiguities in assignments |
| **Brainstorming activities** | Excellent — strong ideation partner |
| **Essay evaluation** | Poor — "isn't great at that" |

Teachers are learning where AI helps and where it wastes time. The useful pattern: AI as a *partner* for generating options, not as a *judge* of quality.

## The Best Practice: Critical AI Literacy

Nicole Haddad (Southern Methodist University) splits it into before/after:

**Before ChatGPT:**
- Discern what's worth knowing
- Figure out how to look it up
- Identify what's worth outsourcing to AI

**After using AI:**
- Think critically about the data
- What might be missing?
- What can be improved?
- How to expand the conversation for richer feedback

This is [[ai-as-management-skill|AI as Management Skill]] applied to education — knowing when to use AI and how to verify its output. Same framework, different domain.

## Student Experiences

**Benefits:**
- "Ask it a million questions and it never gets tired" — Amedeo, 13
- Simplifies difficult concepts
- Generates outlines, examples, practice problems
- Real time saver

**Concerns:**
- "They have industrialized and automated plagiarism" — Travis Huckell
- "The joy of learning is that 'AHA!' moment that comes from figuring something out yourself" — Emma Nazario
- "You don't have to think about a text deeply"

The tension: AI is the best tutor some kids have ever had and the best shortcut others have ever found. Same tool, different use.

## The Equity Gap

> "I foresee an ever yawning gap between the privileged and everyone else, between those who know how to use A.I. as a tool and those who don't know that there is anything to know."
> — Ricardo Galliano Court, Northwestern University

The new digital divide isn't access to computers. It's know-how to use AI effectively. The kid with a laptop and no AI literacy is worse off than the kid with a phone and good AI instincts.

## Detection Reality

> "The short answer is no. The long answer is A.I. use is undetectable."
> — Ethan Mollick, Wharton School

AI detection doesn't work. Asking AI to detect AI produces lies. Every instinct about stopping plagiarism is obsolete. The old homework assignment is "basically cracked by A.I."

## The Assessment Question

> "As a teacher, if I'm asking questions that are easily answered by A.I., am I asking the best questions?"
> — Jennifer Parnell, history teacher

If ChatGPT can answer it, maybe it's not worth asking. Parnell had ChatGPT manufacture an essay, then students analyzed the AI text for errors and rewrote it. Students fed their own essays to AI for feedback on source quality. That's the move: use AI as the *input* to the assignment, not the threat to it.

## Key Insights

1. **Banning failed** — schools that blocked AI are now integrating it
2. **Use cases matter** — AI is great for ideation, poor for evaluation
3. **Literacy over access** — teaching critical AI usage matters more than the tool
4. **Detection is impossible** — stop trying to catch, start adapting
5. **Assessment must evolve** — questions AI can answer are questions worth rethinking
6. **Equity risk** — the new divide is AI know-how, not device access
