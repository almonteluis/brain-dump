# New Classrooms

New Classrooms is the nonprofit behind [[teach-to-one]]. Co-founded by Joel Rose and Chris Rush in 2011, it exists to prove that personalized math instruction — where every kid gets a different daily schedule based on what they actually need — can work in real public schools, at scale, without requiring superhero teachers.

The origin: **School of One**, a 2009 NYC Department of Education pilot. Rose was running innovation at DOE, Rush was his partner. They ran a summer school program where an algorithm assigned each student a daily "playlist" of math activities. The press loved it. *Time* named it one of the 50 best inventions of 2009. Results were promising enough that they spun it out as an independent nonprofit two years later.

## What Makes It Different

Most ed-tech companies build software and sell it to districts. New Classrooms builds an *instructional model* — the technology, the curriculum design, the classroom layout, the teacher professional development, all bundled together. They're not selling an app; they're selling a different way to run a math classroom.

The model depends on three things working together:
1. **The algorithm** — daily playlist generation based on [[knowledge-tracing]] and the [[skill-graph]]
2. **The classroom redesign** — physical spaces with multiple stations for different modalities
3. **The teacher role shift** — from whole-class lecturer to small-group facilitator and data analyst

Take away any one of those and the model doesn't work. That's why it's harder to implement than "install this Chrome extension" — but also why the results, when schools commit, are real.

## The Research Orientation

New Classrooms takes research seriously. The [[competitive-landscape-teach-to-one|competitive landscape]] is full of companies that run an A/B test and call it evidence. NC funds independent evaluations, publishes results (including the [[Unlocking Algebra]] report), and builds its product on learning science — [[spaced-repetition]], [[knowledge-tracing]], [[master-based learning]], [[behavioral-nudges-education]].

[[project-atlas]] is the next bet: layering AI (LLMs, real-time adaptation, multimodal assessment) on top of the existing TTO infrastructure. It's the most ambitious thing they've attempted, and it's where the [[three-ring-mastery-system]] and [[insight-recall]] research connects to actual product decisions.

## Field Observation — USVI Engagement Gap (May 2026)

Source: [[Captain's log, volume one]]

Multiple schools using TTO in the US Virgin Islands. One school shows high student engagement with the program. Others show low or no engagement. The field reasons are not product problems — they're implementation-environment problems:

- **Violence / threats** — school environment too turbulent for consistent program use
- **Staff not trained** — teachers haven't received onboarding or PD for TTO
- **Internet connectivity** — infrastructure can't reliably support the platform

The open question: what is the high-engagement school doing differently? How did they implement TTO that the others didn't? This is a classic implementation-fidelity signal — the product works when the conditions for implementation are met, and those conditions aren't just technical.

This connects to [[differentiated-instruction]] at the system level — you can differentiate the product, but can you differentiate the implementation support?
