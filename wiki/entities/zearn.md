# Zearn

Zearn is the nonprofit that built a top-rated K-8 math curriculum and then let researchers run the largest behavioral nudge experiment in education on its platform. 3 million elementary students. 10,000 teachers. Result: personalized, data-driven email nudges to teachers boosted student math progress by 1.89%. Not nothing, not world-changing — but at that scale, it moves real kids.

The [[paper-nudge-math-megastudy-2025|Duckworth megastudy]] is the main reason Zearn matters in this knowledge base. But it's also a significant player in the [[competitive-landscape-teach-to-one|competitive landscape]] for personalized math. Zearn's approach is different from [[teach-to-one]]: structured digital curriculum with adaptive pathways *within* a fixed scope and sequence, versus TTO's fully algorithmic scheduling across modalities. Zearn gives you a well-designed road; TTO builds the road as you walk it.

## Why It Matters for TTO

Two reasons:

1. **Research platform** — Zearn's scale (1M+ students) makes it a natural testbed for [[behavioral-nudges-education|behavioral nudge]] research, [[paper-streaks-math-engagement|streak mechanics]], and engagement interventions. When a nudge works on Zearn, it's been battle-tested.

2. **Market positioning** — Zearn is free at the core tier. That's a hard price to compete with. TTO competes on depth of personalization and teacher support, not price. Understanding Zearn's model is essential for positioning [[new-classrooms]] in district conversations.

The open thread: Zearn proves you can get meaningful learning gains from relatively lightweight interventions (emails to teachers). [[teach-to-one]] bets that much heavier personalization (algorithmic scheduling, multimodal playlists) produces much larger gains. The evidence for that bet is still being built. If a $0 email nudge moves the needle 1.89%, how much more does a full personalized platform need to move it to justify the cost and complexity? That's the question that keeps [[new-classrooms]] up at night.
