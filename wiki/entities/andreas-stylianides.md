# Andreas Stylianides

Andreas Stylianides is a math education researcher who took the NCTM Learning Principle — "students must learn mathematics with understanding" — and asked the obvious follow-up: *what does that actually mean?* His work on "Learning Mathematics with Understanding" tries to operationalize the gap between performing procedures correctly and genuinely understanding the mathematical ideas behind them.

This is the same distinction that haunts every adaptive learning platform. A kid who gets 100% on a fractions test might understand fractions deeply, or might have memorized a procedure that happens to work for those specific problem types. The difference matters because the procedural kid collapses the moment they see a novel problem; the conceptual kid adapts.

Stylianides' work connects directly to the [[productive-struggle]] debate — NCTM says struggle builds understanding, but *what kind* of struggle? Procedural struggle (trying different algorithms) is different from conceptual struggle (grappling with why the math works). The [[nctm]] practices assume the latter. Most ed-tech products are designed around the former.

His research is a reminder that [[knowledge-tracing]] can track whether a kid got the right answer, but it can't easily distinguish procedural fluency from conceptual understanding. That's the ceiling on current adaptive systems, and it's why [[insight-recall]] and other approaches that surface the kid's *reasoning* (not just their answers) matter for the next generation.
