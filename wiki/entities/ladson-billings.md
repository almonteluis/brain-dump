# Gloria Ladson-Billings

Gloria [[ladson-billings]] developed the framework of **culturally relevant pedagogy** (CRP) — later refined as culturally sustaining pedagogy. Her core argument: students' cultural backgrounds are assets for learning, not obstacles to work around. Effective teaching affirms and leverages cultural identity rather than erasing it.

## The 1995 Paper

*Toward a Theory of Culturally Relevant Pedagogy* established three pillars:

1. **Academic Achievement** — All students held to high intellectual standards and supported in meeting them. No lowering the bar.
2. **Cultural Competence** — Students develop competence in their own culture and at least one other. Not assimilation — bilingualism.
3. **Sociopolitical Consciousness** — Students learn to identify, critique, and address social inequities. Education is political whether you acknowledge it or not.

## Core Ideas

- **Deficit thinking is the enemy** — framing students' backgrounds as deficits rather than assets is the root of inequitable outcomes
- **Teachers must know their students** — culturally relevant teaching requires deep knowledge of students' communities and experiences
- **Excellence and equity are not in tension** — rigorous academics and cultural affirmation are complementary, not competing goals
- **Education is inherently political** — teaching that ignores sociopolitical context fails to prepare students for full civic participation

## Relevance to CBL and TTO

- **Competency Collaborative (2023)** — applied Ladson-Billings' framework to CBE in NYC schools. CBE's transparency, revision opportunities, and flexible pacing particularly benefit marginalized students.
- **Aurora Institute (2020)** — equity strategies for CBE draw on CRP principles: transparent targets, flexible pacing, multiple pathways.
- **[[math-identity]] research** — Ladson-Billings' work underpins research showing that identity is co-constructed through culturally responsive teacher-student interactions.

For TTO: the algorithm can pick the right problem, but it can't build the trust that makes a kid *willing to struggle* with that problem. That's still the teacher's job. [[culturally-responsive-pedagogy|CRP]] is the framework for making sure the platform supports that relationship instead of crowding it out.
