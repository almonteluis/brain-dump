# Xuefei Hou

Xuefei Hou is the researcher who, with [[xizhao-tan]], asked a question that should've been obvious: why do spaced repetition systems only surface *content* for review? What if they surfaced the *learner's own thinking* instead?

That question became the **Insight Recall (IRec)** paper (2025). The pitch: learning systems capture student insights all the time (answers, reflections, strategy notes) and then throw them away. IRec says: store those insights, index them, and surface them at contextually relevant moments — not on a fixed schedule, but when the *situation* calls for them. It's metacognitive scaffolding that the student built for themselves, delivered back at the moment it matters.

This isn't a small tweak to [[spaced-repetition]]. It's a paradigm shift. Traditional SRS says "review this fact before you forget it." IRec says "here's what you were thinking last time you encountered something like this — does it help now?" The retrieval cue is the situation, not a timer.

Hou's work sits right at the intersection of three threads in the TTO knowledge base: it addresses the [[encoding-specificity-principle]] problem (de-contextualized review is weak review), it feeds directly into the [[three-ring-mastery-system]]'s maintenance ring design, and it's one of the research pillars for [[project-atlas]]. The insight-recall approach is one of the few genuinely new ideas in adaptive learning that doesn't just amount to "add more data to the model."

[[insight-recall]] has the full concept breakdown. The open thread: if IRec surfaces the student's own thinking, what happens when that thinking was *wrong*? Surfacing a misconception at the wrong moment could reinforce it. That's the tension between metacognitive scaffolding and misconception reinforcement — and it's where [[knowledge-tracing]] needs to be accurate enough to know the difference.
