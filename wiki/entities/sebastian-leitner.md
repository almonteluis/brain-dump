# Sebastian Leitner

Sebastian Leitner (1919–1989) was a German science journalist who figured out that you could operationalize [[spaced-repetition]] with nothing but flashcards and a few boxes. His system, published in *So lernt man lernen* (1972), is the low-tech ancestor of every SRS app running today — Anki, [[duolingo]]'s scheduler, the whole lineage.

## The Leitner System

Here's the setup: you've got 5 boxes and a stack of flashcards. Every card starts in Box 1.

- **Box 1** → review daily. Get it right → move to Box 2.
- **Box 2** → review every 2 days. Get it right → move to Box 3.
- **Box 3** → review weekly. Get it right → move to Box 4.
- **Box 4** → review biweekly. Get it right → Box 5.
- **Box 5** → review monthly. This is the "I own this" box.

**Miss at any level?** Back to Box 1. No mercy.

The elegance: hard cards (the ones you keep missing) get reviewed constantly. Easy cards fade into the background and only surface when the interval's long enough that you might actually need the refresh. It's self-organizing. The cards tell you what to study by where they are in the system.

## Where He Sits in the Lineage

| Generation | Who | What they added |
|------------|-----|----------------|
| Discovery | [[hermann-ebbinghaus]] | "Forgetting exists, and spacing slows it down" (1885) |
| First algorithm | [[paul-pimsleur]] | Scheduled intervals for language vocab (1967) |
| **Democratization** | **Leitner** | **Made it physical, simple, and free** (1972) |
| Computer era | Wozniak (SuperMemo) | SM-2 algorithm, software-based SRS (1987) |
| Data-driven | [[burr-settles]] | Half-Life Regression, learned from 12M+ data points (2016) |

Leitner is the bridge between "interesting lab finding" and "thing actual humans can do." Without him, spaced repetition stays in academic papers. With him, it's a shoebox on your desk.

## Why He Matters for TTO

The [[three-ring-mastery-system]]'s maintenance ring is doing something Leitner would recognize: skills get reviewed less frequently as mastery solidifies, and they snap back to high-frequency review if the student slips. The [[cooldown-system]] is the digital version of "move it back to Box 1." The difference is that TTO's "boxes" are driven by [[knowledge-tracing]] estimates, not fixed time intervals — but the spirit is Leitner's.

The limitation Leitner couldn't solve: his system works on *facts*. Vocabulary, capitals, anatomy terms. It doesn't know what to do with conceptual understanding, problem-solving strategies, or the difference between "can recite the formula" and "knows when to use it." That's the gap [[insight-recall]] is trying to fill — surfacing the student's own reasoning, not just their flashcard recall.
