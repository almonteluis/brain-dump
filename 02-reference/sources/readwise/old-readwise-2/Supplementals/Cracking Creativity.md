Creative geniuses do not wait for the gifts of chance; instead, they actively seek the accidental discovery. ^928816159

Always try to rephrase a problem in your own words, without using definitions. ^928816160

Einstein was once asked what the difference was between him and the average person. He said that if you asked the average person to find a needle in a haystack, the person would stop when he or she found a needle. He, on the other hand, would tear through the entire haystack looking for all the possible needles. ^928816161

A number of experiments have shown the significance of language for problem solving. Adults who write out or verbalize problems perform far better than those who silently go about the job. ^928816162

Before you brainstorm any problem, restate the problem at least five to ten times to generate multiple perspectives. The emphasis is not so much on the right problem definition but on alternative problem definitions. Sooner or later, you’ll find one that you are comfortable with. ^928816163

---

When confronted with a problem, they ask themselves how many different ways they can look the problem, how they can rethink it, and how many different ways they can solve it, instead of asking how they have been taught to solve it. ^928816164

---

The more specifically you define your challenge, the easier it is to generate ideas. Ask who, what, where, when, why, and how. ^928816165

---

A cataract builds over time and its effects become obvious slowly, because the change goes almost unnoticed until the cataract reduces vision significantly. Likewise, the habits and routines with which we approach problems gradually accumulate until they significantly reduce our awareness of other possibilities. ^928816166
