## 🎯 Why This Matters

Traditional mathematics teaching is about solving exactly stated problems exactly. But life often hands us partly defined problems needing only moderately accurate solutions. This book/course teaches art of educated guessing and down-and-dirty, opportunistic problem solving.

**The philosophy:** In problem solving, as in street fighting, rules are for fools: do whatever works — don't just stand there! Yet we often fear an unjustified leap even though it may land us on a correct result.

---

## 📋 Key Insights

### Insight 1: Traditional Rigor Mortis
Too much mathematical rigor prevents real problem solving. We're trained to want exact calculations and formal proofs, but life presents partly defined problems where exactness is impossible or too expensive.

**The antidote:** Educated guessing — approximate, good-enough solutions that solve problem at hand.

### Insight 2: Six Tools for Educated Guessing

Mahajan describes six tools for opportunistic problem solving:

1. **Dimensional Analysis** — Check units and consistency
2. **Easy Cases** — Test simple scenarios first
3. **Lumping** — Group similar quantities together
4. **Picture Proofs** — Visual reasoning
5. **Successive Approximation** — Refine estimates iteratively
6. **Reasoning by Analogy** — Use similar problems as guides

**Key principle:** He separates tool (the general principle) from particular application so reader can easily grasp tool itself and use it on problems of particular interest.

### Insight 3: Rules Are For Fools

Street fighting metaphor: In a fight, you don't follow formal rules — you do whatever works. Same for problem solving.

**The problem:** We often fear an unjustified leap even though it may land on a correct result.

**The solution:** Do whatever works. Don't just stand there.

### Insight 4: Partly Defined Problems

Life hands us partly defined problems needing only moderately accurate solutions. Traditional math teaching doesn't prepare us for this.

**Example:** Estimating server capacity, projecting user growth, sizing a database — these don't need exact answers, but they need educated guesses that are close enough to make decisions.

### Insight 5: Tool Reuse Across Domains

Street-Fighting Mathematics grew out of an MIT course for students ranging from first-year undergraduates to graduates ready for careers in physics, mathematics, management, electrical engineering, computer science, and biology.

**Key insight:** Tools apply across diverse fields. Dimensional analysis isn't just for physics — it works for any domain with measurable quantities.

## 💡 New Concepts

### Concept: Educated Guessing

Making estimates and approximations that are "good enough" for decision-making without needing exact calculations or formal proofs.

**Why it matters:**
- Exact solutions are often impossible or too expensive
- Partly defined problems are common in real life
- Fast, approximate beats slow, perfect for most decisions

**When to use:** Estimations, back-of-the-envelope calculations, sizing, capacity planning, projections.

### Concept: Opportunistic Problem Solving

Taking advantage of whatever tools, approximations, or shortcuts are available to solve a problem at hand, rather than following formal procedures.

**Why it matters:**
- Real-world problems often lack complete information
- Following formal procedures when they don't apply wastes time
- "Good enough" solutions enable progress; exact solutions prevent progress

**When to use:** Any real-world problem with incomplete or partly defined information.

## 🔗 Frameworks & Patterns

### Street-Fighting Mathematics Toolkit

**Description:** Six tools for educated guessing and opportunistic problem solving applicable across diverse domains.

#### Tool 1: Dimensional Analysis
**What it does:** Check units, consistency, and dependencies by analyzing dimensions

**Example:** If X ∝ T⁴, dimensional analysis shows what X could be (area, volume, etc.)

**Application:** Checking formulas, catching errors, estimating unknowns

**When to use:** Any problem with measurable quantities

#### Tool 2: Easy Cases
**What it does:** Test simple scenarios first (extreme cases, zero, one, infinity)

**Example:** Check if formula works when n=0 or n=1 before trusting general n

**Application:** Testing edge cases, validating formulas, finding patterns

**When to use:** Complex formulas, new algorithms, edge case testing

#### Tool 3: Lumping
**What it does:** Group similar quantities together to simplify estimation

**Example:** Instead of tracking each user individually, lump into cohorts (active, churned, new)

**Application:** Quick estimations, capacity planning, rough sizing

**When to use:** Complex systems with many similar components

#### Tool 4: Picture Proofs
**What it does:** Visual reasoning with diagrams, graphs, or illustrations

**Example:** Prove geometric relationship with drawing instead of algebra

**Application:** Architecture design, data flow visualization, explaining concepts

**When to use:** When visualization reveals patterns or contradictions

#### Tool 5: Successive Approximation
**What it does:** Start with rough estimate, then refine iteratively

**Example:** First estimate 10⁶ ≈ 1000, then refine to 1,048,576

**Application:** Iterative problem solving, improving estimates over time

**When to use:** Complex calculations, multi-step problems

#### Tool 6: Reasoning by Analogy
**What it does:** Use similar problems or domains as guides for current problem

**Example:** Electrical circuit problems solved by analogy to fluid flow

**Application:** Cross-domain learning, explaining concepts, pattern recognition

**When to use:** When problem resembles another problem you understand

**How to apply:**
1. Identify problem type
2. Choose applicable tool(s)
3. Apply tool to get educated guess
4. Check if result is reasonable
5. Refine if needed

**When to use:** Any problem where exact calculation is impossible, too expensive, or unnecessary.
